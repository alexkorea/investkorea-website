## Complete Guide to Real Estate Acquisition Tax and Capital Gains Tax for Foreigners in Korea 2026

When a foreigner acquires real estate in Korea, **acquisition tax, rural development tax, and local education tax** must be paid immediately. When the property is later sold, **capital gains tax (CGT)** applies. Non-resident foreigners are subject to special tax rates different from those for Korean residents, so understanding the full tax structure before signing any contract is essential.

This guide explains the 2026 tax rate framework, reporting deadlines, calculation examples, and common mistakes — step by step.

## 1. Acquisition Tax: Basic Rates

Acquisition tax rates vary by the reason for acquisition (purchase, gift, inheritance, etc.) and property type. For standard residential home purchases in 2026:

| Acquisition Method | House (under KRW 600M) | House (KRW 600M–900M) | House (over KRW 900M) | Land / Commercial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase | 1% | 1~3% (graduated) | 3% | 4% |
| Gift | 3.5% | 3.5% | 3.5% | 3.5% |
| Inheritance | 2.8% | 2.8% | 2.8% | 2.8% |

In addition to acquisition tax, **rural development tax** (10% of acquisition tax) and **local education tax** (20% of acquisition tax) are added. The effective tax burden is approximately 1.3 times the base acquisition tax rate.

The tax calculation method is the same for foreigners as for Korean nationals. However, the **foreign real estate acquisition report** (Bank of Korea) and the **acquisition tax filing** (district office) are separate procedures and must not be confused.

## 2. Regulated Areas and Multi-Home Heavy Tax Rates

If the property is located in a government-designated "regulated area" or the buyer qualifies as a multi-home owner, heavy tax rates apply.

| Category | 2nd home in regulated area | 3rd+ home in regulated area | Corporate acquisition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition tax rate | 8% | 12% | 12% |

- The current list of regulated areas is determined by Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport notices and changes based on the date of acquisition.
- Foreign corporations acquiring residential property are generally subject to the 12% rate.
- Temporary two-home exceptions (if the previous home is sold within 3 years) may exempt buyers from the heavy rate, but foreign nationals must also meet additional residence and visa conditions.

> **Warning:** Regulated area designations change frequently. Always confirm the current status with the district office or the Ministry's real transaction price portal (rt.molit.go.kr) before signing a contract.

## 3. VAT Applicability

Residential housing is **VAT-exempt**. However, commercial properties, offices, and some land transactions may be subject to VAT.

| Property Type | VAT |
|---|---|
| Residential housing | Exempt |
| Commercial / office | Taxable (10% of supply price) |
| Land | Exempt |
| Pre-sale rights (business use) | Potentially taxable |

If acquiring commercial property, confirm in advance whether input VAT credits are available. Foreign buyers who purchase commercial property without a registered business in Korea cannot claim input VAT deductions — consulting a tax professional before acquisition is strongly recommended.

## 4. Capital Gains Tax Rate Structure

Capital gains tax applies to profits from selling Korean real estate. Rates depend on **holding period** and **number of homes owned**.

### General CGT Rates (Resident Basis)

| Taxable Income | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to KRW 14M | 6% |
| KRW 14M–50M | 15% |
| KRW 50M–88M | 24% |
| KRW 88M–150M | 35% |
| KRW 150M–300M | 38% |
| KRW 300M–500M | 40% |
| Over KRW 500M | 42% |

### Short-Term Holding Heavy Rates

| Holding Period | Residential Rate | Pre-Sale Rights Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1 year | 70% | 70% |
| 1–2 years | 60% | 60% |
| 2+ years | Standard rates | — |

The long-term holding deduction applies after **3 years of holding** — up to 80% for one-home residents. However, non-resident foreigners face significant restrictions on the one-home CGT exemption and long-term holding deductions.

## 5. Special Rates for Non-Resident Foreigners

**Non-residents** — foreigners who have stayed in Korea for less than one year or whose primary livelihood is based abroad — are subject to a separate tax framework.

### Key Rules for Non-Resident CGT

- **No one-home exemption:** Non-residents rarely meet the 2-year residency requirement, so the exemption generally does not apply.
- **Restricted long-term holding deduction:** Non-residents are capped at approximately 30% deduction even after 3+ years of holding, versus the resident maximum of 80%.
- **Withholding tax obligation:** Buyers purchasing from a non-resident foreign seller must withhold a portion of the purchase price. The withholding rate is generally the lower of 10% of the sale price or 20% of the gain (subject to variation — confirm with the local tax office).
- **Tax treaty application:** Korea has treaties with the US, China, Japan, and many other countries. The treaty may affect which country has taxing rights, but in practice Korea generally retains the right to tax Korean-source real estate gains. Each case requires individual review.

> **Practical note:** Whether a person qualifies as a resident or non-resident depends on days of stay, income source, and center of life — and tax office judgments may vary. Always confirm your status with a Korean tax advisor before acquiring property.

## 6. Reporting Deadlines and Procedures

### Acquisition Tax Filing Deadline

| Acquisition Method | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Purchase / exchange | Within 60 days of acquisition |
| Gift | Within 60 days of acquisition |
| Inheritance | Within 6 months from the last day of the month of the inheritance |

File at the **district (si/gun/gu) office** where the property is located. A 20% non-filing penalty applies if the deadline is missed.

### Foreign Real Estate Acquisition Report (Bank of Korea)

In addition to acquisition tax, **Foreign Exchange Transaction Act** reporting is required when a foreigner acquires Korean real estate.

- Reporting authority: Foreign exchange bank or Bank of Korea
- Timing: After contract but before or at acquisition (advance report is standard)
- Failure to report may result in fines under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act.

### CGT Reporting Deadlines

| Type | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Preliminary report | Within 2 months from the last day of the month of sale |
| Final return | May 1–31 of the following year |

Non-resident foreigners must file a final return even if they did not file a preliminary report. Late payment attracts additional penalties.

## 8. Common Mistakes and Warnings

### Frequent Errors Made by Foreigners

1. **Omitting the Bank of Korea report:** Paying acquisition tax but forgetting the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act report — subject to fines.
2. **Misclassifying residency status:** Claiming resident CGT treatment as a non-resident, leading to back-tax assessments.
3. **Missing regulated area changes:** The area designation can change between contract date and actual acquisition, triggering heavy tax rates.
4. **Missing the preliminary CGT report:** Not filing the 2-month preliminary report leads to penalty surcharges.
5. **Buyer unaware of withholding obligation:** Buyer pays the full price to a non-resident seller without withholding, creating a tax compliance issue.

### Pre-Acquisition Checklist

- [ ] Confirm whether the property is in a regulated area
- [ ] Verify resident vs. non-resident status with a tax advisor
- [ ] File Foreign Exchange Transaction Act report (Bank of Korea / exchange bank)
- [ ] File and pay acquisition tax within 60 days of acquisition (district office)
- [ ] File preliminary CGT report within 2 months of sale
- [ ] Review applicable tax treaty provisions

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can foreigners freely purchase real estate in Korea?**

A: In principle, yes. However, certain properties — land in transaction-permit zones, farmland, and military protection areas — require special permits or face restrictions. Always check the regulatory status of the specific property before acquisition.

**Q2. Can non-resident foreigners claim the long-term holding deduction?**

A: Yes, but at a lower rate than residents. Non-residents are generally capped at around 30% even after holding 3+ years, compared to the resident maximum of 80%. Practical application may vary — confirm with a tax advisor.

**Q3. Where do foreigners file the real estate acquisition report?**

A: Under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act, report to a foreign exchange bank (your primary bank) or the Bank of Korea. The acquisition tax filing is a separate process at the district office where the property is located.

**Q4. Does a tax treaty mean no CGT in Korea?**

A: Not necessarily. Korea has treaties with the US, China, Japan, and others, but Korea generally retains the right to tax gains from Korean real estate. Treaty terms vary, and each case needs individual review.

**Q5. What happens if I file acquisition tax late?**

A: Filing after the 60-day deadline triggers a 20% non-filing penalty, plus a late-payment penalty if taxes are not paid on time. File as early as possible to avoid surcharges.
