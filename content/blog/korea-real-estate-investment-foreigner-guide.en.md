## Can Foreigners Buy Real Estate in Korea?

Yes. Korea generally permits foreign nationals to purchase real estate, including residential, commercial, and land properties. However, the Act on Report on Real Estate Transactions requires foreign buyers to complete an acquisition report within 60 days of signing the contract.

Certain restricted zones (military protection areas, cultural heritage zones) require advance government approval.

## Step-by-Step Purchase Process

### 1. Pre-Purchase Due Diligence
- Review the property registry: check ownership, mortgages, and injunctions
- Check land use zoning, floor-area ratio, and building regulations
- Confirm there are no prior tenants with registered lease rights

### 2. Contract and Deposit
Pay a deposit (typically 10% of the purchase price) upon signing. Both parties must verify identity (passport required for foreign buyers).

### 3. Acquisition Report
Within 60 days of the contract date, report the acquisition to the local district office. Required documents: copy of purchase contract, passport copy, and proof of remittance.

### 4. Final Payment and Ownership Registration
Pay the balance and simultaneously apply for ownership transfer registration at the district court registry. The acquisition report confirmation is required for this step.

## Key Taxes

| Tax | Rate | Due |
|-----|------|-----|
| Acquisition Tax | 1–12% (varies by price and units owned) | Within 60 days of acquisition |
| Local Education Tax | 20% of acquisition tax | Same as acquisition tax |
| Rural Special Tax | 10% of acquisition tax (above 85 sqm) | Same as acquisition tax |

## Overseas Remittance

Transfers exceeding USD 10,000 require proof of fund source under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Coordinate with a Korean foreign exchange bank before large transfers.
