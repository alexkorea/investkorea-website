## Branch vs. Subsidiary: Which Should You Choose?

Foreign companies entering the Korean market can choose between a **branch office** (no separate legal entity, parent liable) or a **subsidiary** (independent legal entity, limited liability). A branch is cheaper and simpler to establish, making it ideal for market testing or limited-scope operations.

## Step-by-Step Branch Registration Process

### Step 1: Business Feasibility and Industry Check
Verify that your industry is not on Korea's negative list for foreign investment. Regulated sectors (finance, insurance, telecoms) require additional licensing.

### Step 2: Prepare Parent Company Documents
- Articles of Incorporation
- Board resolution approving the Korean branch
- Notarized signature of the authorized representative
- Apostille or consular legalization

### Step 3: Court Registry Filing
File a branch registration application at the Korean district court registry. Appoint a branch manager (who need not be Korean). Obtain a certified copy of the branch registry.

### Step 4: Tax Office Business Registration
Register with the local tax office within 20 days of commencing business. Choose between general VAT taxpayer or simplified taxpayer status.

### Step 5: Foreign Investment Notification
For investments of KRW 100 million or more, notify the foreign exchange bank or KOTRA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. This entitles the branch to tax incentives and location support.

### Step 6: Banking and Social Insurance
Open a corporate bank account (domestic or foreign bank). Enroll in Korea's four social insurance programs once employees are hired.

## Timeline

Total process typically takes **5–8 weeks** from document preparation to operational status.
