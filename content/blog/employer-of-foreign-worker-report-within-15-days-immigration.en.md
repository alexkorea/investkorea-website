The company must report to the immigration office within 15 days. The Immigration Act requires anyone who employs a foreigner holding a status of stay that permits employment to report when the foreigner is dismissed, resigns or dies, when the whereabouts of the employed foreigner become unknown, or when important terms of the employment contract are changed. This is the company's own duty, separate from any report the foreigner makes.

This guide is for foreign-invested companies with foreign staff and for HR and administration managers at Korean companies. Companies often leave immigration matters to the foreign employees themselves and miss the company-side report, so it is worth setting out the deadline for each ground.

## Key Summary

- Who must report: anyone who employs a foreigner holding a status of stay that permits employment (the same applies to the head of a company providing industrial technical training to foreigners)
- Grounds: dismissal, resignation or death / whereabouts unknown / change of important terms of the employment contract
- Deadline: within 15 days from the start date for each ground (for resignation or dismissal, that day; for death or unknown whereabouts, the day you learned of it; for a contract change, the day of the change)
- Where: the head of the Immigration Office, office or branch office
- Form: report on the occurrence of a change concerning employed or trained foreigners
- Some changes, such as a change of a corporation's representative, are excluded from important changes
- Breach of the reporting duty is subject to a fine for negligence, and neglecting it twice or more in the last 1 month can disadvantage the company when inviting foreigners

## 1. Who must report?

A foreigner who wants to work in Korea must obtain a status of stay that permits employment. The person who employs a foreigner holding such a status is the one obliged to report. The law refers simply to "a person who employs a foreigner", so foreign-invested companies have the same duty.

The same provision applies mutatis mutandis to the head of a company providing industrial technical training to foreigners. Even if the foreigner has separate procedures of their own, such as reporting changes to alien registration details, the employer's report must be handled separately.

## 2. What triggers a report? Three grounds

| Ground | Details |
|---|---|
| Dismissal, resignation, death | The foreigner is dismissed, or the foreigner resigns or dies |
| Whereabouts unknown | The whereabouts of the employed foreigner become unknown |
| Contract change | Important terms of the employment contract are changed |

Continued unauthorized absence with no contact is a typical example of unknown whereabouts. See section 5 below for what to include when reporting unknown whereabouts.

## 3. What is a "change of important terms of the employment contract"?

The Enforcement Decree treats the following as important changes:

- The employment contract period is changed
- The employer or representative changes, the name of the workplace changes, or the location changes because the workplace moves
- Workers are dispatched under other laws such as the Act on the Protection, etc. of Temporary Agency Workers (including a change of the dispatch workplace)

However, the following are excluded from the second item:

- A foreigner is employed by a State agency or local government
- A foreigner is employed by a school under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act or the Higher Education Act, or a higher education institution under a special act
- The representative of a corporation changes
- The foreigner changes workplace through the workplace change procedure

If a foreign-invested company moves its head office or changes its trade name, this may count as a change in workplace location or name. A change of the corporation's representative director, on the other hand, is an exclusion.

## 4. When do the 15 days start?

| Ground | Start date of the 15 days |
|---|---|
| Dismissal or resignation | The day the foreigner was dismissed or resigned |
| Death | The day you learned the foreigner died |
| Whereabouts unknown | The day you learned the foreigner's whereabouts were unknown |
| Change of important contract terms | The day the important terms of the employment contract were changed |

Resignation is counted from the day of resignation, not from the day you learned of it. If departures are shared late in your company, it is safer to build the report schedule into the HR process.

## 5. Where and how is the report filed?

The report is filed with the head of the Immigration Office, office or branch office, by submitting the report on the occurrence of a change concerning employed or trained foreigners.

The employment change guidance for Non-professional Employment (E-9) includes the following:

- Reports can be made in person at the immigration office with jurisdiction over the place of stay, by fax, or through HiKorea e-civil services; fax and e-civil reports are processed only if the reporting deadline has not passed
- HiKorea e-civil reporting is integrated with the Ministry of Employment and Labor's employment change report
- Documents: report on the occurrence of a change concerning employed or trained foreigners, copy of the alien registration card (for an unknown-whereabouts report, the person's mobile phone number, expected location and so on must be included), copy of the business registration certificate, the employer's ID (when the employer reports in person), and for a filing by a company employee, the employer's power of attorney, certificate of employment and the employee's ID
- Resignation due to the expiry of the contract period does not need to be reported

Because this guidance is for E-9 workers, whether the same methods and criteria apply to staff with other statuses, such as Corporate Investment (D-8) or Special Occupation (E-7), needs to be confirmed case by case. An employer may also have this report filed by an agency that handles stay-related applications for foreigners.

## 6. What happens after the report?

The office receiving the report investigates the facts without delay and takes the necessary measures. Immigration officers and others may investigate the facts if there are reasonable grounds to suspect the report does not match the facts.

If an employer of a foreigner covered by the Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers files this report and the facts also constitute a reporting ground under that Act, the report under that Act is deemed to have been made as well. In that case the office notifies, without delay, the head of the employment security office with jurisdiction over the employer's location.

## 7. What if you do not report? Common problems

An employer who breaches the reporting duty is subject to a fine for negligence. In addition, if a person who has neglected this reporting duty twice or more in the last 1 month from the application date invites a foreigner, the certificate for confirmation of visa issuance may not be issued. The specific fine amount needs to be confirmed case by case.

Common problems:

- Thinking the company has nothing to do because the foreign employee has left Korea or changed status
- Counting the 15 days from the day the paperwork was settled rather than the day of resignation
- Missing reports for foreign staff when moving the head office or changing the trade name
- Waiting too long for an employee who stopped responding and missing the time to report unknown whereabouts
- Putting off several reports at once and missing them repeatedly within a short period

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must a foreign-invested company also file this report?

The law places the reporting duty on anyone who employs a foreigner holding a status of stay that permits employment, so a foreign-invested company that employs foreigners has the same duty.

### By when must I report if an employee resigns?

Report to the head of the Immigration Office, office or branch office within 15 days from the day the foreigner was dismissed or resigned.

### Must we report if the representative director changes?

A change of employer or representative is an important change, but a change of a corporation's representative is excluded. A change of workplace name or relocation is a reporting ground.

### We lost contact with an employee. When do the 15 days start?

Report within 15 days from the day you learned that the foreigner's whereabouts were unknown.

### What are the consequences of missing a report?

The employer becomes subject to a fine for negligence, and neglecting the reporting duty twice or more in the last 1 month may mean a certificate for confirmation of visa issuance is not issued when inviting foreigners later.
