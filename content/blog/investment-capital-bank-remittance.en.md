To incorporate a company in Korea, foreign investors must deposit capital funds into a Korean bank account. The challenge: before the company is incorporated, there is no corporate account yet. Here is how to resolve this.

---

## Capital Remittance Process Order

1. **Open a personal account in the founder's name** — before incorporation, use the founder's personal bank account
2. **Remit capital from overseas to the domestic account**
3. **Foreign exchange report** — document the source and purpose of funds
4. **Obtain capital deposit certificate** — bank issues a "Share Payment Deposit Confirmation" or balance certificate
5. **Register the company** — submit the certificate with registration documents
6. **Open corporate account** — after company registration is complete

---

## Foreign Exchange Reporting Requirements

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, remittances to Korea are subject to reporting requirements.

**Threshold:** Individual transfers exceeding USD 10,000

**How to report:**
- Handled at the overseas sending bank (when sending from abroad)
- At a domestic bank window in some cases when receiving domestically

> **Practical caution:** Clearly state the purpose of the remittance. Unexplained large deposits may be flagged as suspicious transactions and result in account holds. Always specify `Capital investment for company incorporation` in the remittance details.

---

## Capital Recognition Standards

To obtain the bank's capital deposit certificate, the funds must actually be in the account.

- **Recognition date:** The date the funds appear in the account
- **Foreign currency:** Converted to Korean won at the day's exchange rate
- **Minimum capital:** KRW 100 million or more (required for foreign investment registration under FIPA)

> **Practical tip:** During periods of significant exchange rate fluctuation, the converted amount may fall short of KRW 100 million. Remit with a buffer to account for rate movements.

## Key Cautions

- **Do not mix capital funds with personal expenses.** Capital must be kept separate. Co-mingling makes it impossible to prove the deposit.
- **No virtual accounts or instant transfer services.** Capital must be remitted via standard international bank wire (SWIFT).
- **Do not withdraw before incorporation.** Withdrawing the capital before company registration invalidates the deposit requirement.

---

## FAQ

**Q. Can I open a Korean bank account before the company is incorporated?**
A. Yes. Foreign nationals can open personal bank accounts. You will need your Alien Registration Card (or passport) and proof of residency status.

**Q. Can I receive the funds in a friend's or family member's account?**
A. No. Funds received in a third party's account cannot be used to prove capital payment. The founder must receive funds in their own name.

**Q. Can the funds be in foreign currency, or do they need to be in Korean won?**
A. Foreign currency is acceptable. The bank will convert to Korean won at the day's exchange rate and issue the certificate accordingly.
