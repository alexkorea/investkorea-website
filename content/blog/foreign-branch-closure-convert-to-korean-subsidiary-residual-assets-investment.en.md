Yes. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act lists, as one type of contribution object, the remaining assets distributed to a foreigner upon liquidation of a branch or office in Korea when the foreigner closes it and converts it into another Korean corporation. This means a foreign investment that acquires shares in the new corporation with the assets left in the branch is possible without sending new money from abroad.

This guide is for foreign companies that have operated a branch or office in Korea and want to wind it up and continue the business through a Korean corporation (subsidiary). Only the form of contribution differs; the foreign investment requirements and the reporting and registration duties still apply.

## Key Summary

- Contribution objects include means of external payment, capital goods, fruits of shares, industrial property rights and similar technology, remaining assets on branch or office closure or dissolution of a Korean corporation, repayments of loans, shares, and real estate in Korea
- Remaining assets distributed to the foreigner upon liquidation when a branch or office is closed and converted into another Korean corporation qualify as a contribution object
- A share-acquisition foreign investment requires an investment amount of KRW 100 million or more and ownership of 10% or more of voting shares (or dispatching or appointing an officer)
- Acquiring shares of a corporation being established also counts as foreign investment
- The investment must be reported in advance and a report certificate is issued (filings are delegated to the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and foreign exchange banks)
- Register as a foreign-invested company within 60 days after completing payment of the contribution object
- Check the registration, tax and foreign exchange procedures for liquidating the branch, and the staff's immigration status, separately

## 1. Why can a branch's remaining assets serve as capital?

The Foreign Investment Promotion Act calls what a foreign investor contributes to own shares a "contribution object" and specifies its types.

| Type of contribution object | Details |
|---|---|
| Means of external payment | Means of external payment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, or domestic means of payment obtained by exchanging them |
| Capital goods | Machinery, equipment and the like as industrial facilities |
| Fruits | Fruits arising from shares acquired under the Act |
| Technology | Industrial property rights, intellectual property rights, equivalent technology and rights to use them |
| Remaining assets on liquidation | Remaining assets distributed to the foreigner upon liquidation when a branch or office is closed and converted into another Korean corporation, or when a Korean corporation in which the foreigner owns shares is dissolved |
| Others | Repayments of loans and other borrowings from abroad, shares prescribed by Presidential Decree, real estate in Korea, and more |

When a branch is wound up and the business moves to a new Korean corporation, the Act allows the remaining assets that return to the foreigner (head office) through the branch liquidation to be contributed to the new corporation.

## 2. What requirements apply for it to count as foreign investment?

Even with a residual-asset contribution, the requirements for a share-acquisition foreign investment are the same.

- The investment amount must be KRW 100 million or more
- The foreigner must own 10% or more of the total voting shares or total capital contributions issued by the Korean corporation (including one being established), or own shares and dispatch or appoint an officer to that corporation

When two or more foreigners invest together, the investment amount is counted per person. How much the remaining assets turn out to be depends on the liquidation, so whether the KRW 100 million requirement is met needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 3. When and where is the report filed?

A foreigner who intends to make a foreign investment must report it in advance to the Minister of Trade and Industry, and a report certificate is issued without delay once the report is received. Receiving reports and change reports and issuing report certificates are delegated to the heads of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and foreign exchange banks, so in practice filings go to these institutions.

If reported items such as the foreign investment ratio and amount, the trade name or address of the foreign investor or foreign-invested company, or the business to be operated change, a change report can be filed. Since the amount to be contributed may differ from the original report after liquidation, it is worth keeping this in mind.

## 4. How is the foreign-invested company registered after payment?

A foreign investor or foreign-invested company must register as a foreign-invested company once payment of the contribution object is complete, within 60 days from the date that event occurs. The application goes to the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency or a foreign exchange bank (the entrusted institution), with documents such as the corporate registration certificate, shareholder register and a copy of the business registration certificate. If the registration requirements are met, a foreign-invested company registration certificate is issued.

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| (1) Advance report | Report the foreign investment before contributing and receive the report certificate |
| (2) Liquidation and contribution | Contribute the remaining assets distributed through liquidation of the branch or office to the new corporation |
| (3) Registration | Register as a foreign-invested company within 60 days of completing payment and receive the registration certificate |
| (4) Changes | If registered items change, file a change registration within 60 days of the event |

The supporting documents to attach when contributing remaining assets may vary with the form of contribution, so it is best to check with the entrusted institution in advance.

## 5. What about the branch liquidation and staff immigration status?

Closing and liquidating a branch or office involves procedures under other laws, such as registration, tax and foreign exchange reporting, which run separately from the foreign investment procedure described here. Foreign staff who worked at the branch will have a different workplace and employer entity, so whether a change of status or a workplace-related report is needed should be confirmed case by case.

## 6. What are the common problems?

- Assuming no foreign investment report is needed because a residual-asset contribution is not an overseas remittance
- Not preparing another contribution method when the remaining assets fall short of KRW 100 million
- Not registering as a foreign-invested company within 60 days after completing the contribution
- Not considering a change report when the liquidation amount differs from the original report
- Postponing the staff's immigration status arrangements until after the corporation is set up

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can branch assets be used as capital for the new corporation?

When a branch or office is closed and converted into another Korean corporation, the remaining assets distributed to the foreigner through liquidation are a contribution object under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

### Is a foreign investment report still required for a residual-asset contribution?

A foreign investment must be reported in advance, and this duty is the same regardless of the type of contribution object. The report certificate is issued without delay after the report.

### What if the amount is less than KRW 100 million?

A share-acquisition foreign investment requires an investment amount of KRW 100 million or more, so if the remaining assets fall short, options such as combining other contribution objects need to be confirmed case by case.

### By when must the company be registered?

Registration as a foreign-invested company must be made with the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency or a foreign exchange bank within 60 days of completing payment of the contribution object.

### Do remaining assets on dissolution of a Korean corporation also qualify?

Yes. When a Korean corporation in which the foreigner owns shares is dissolved, the remaining assets distributed to the foreigner through its liquidation are also a contribution object.
