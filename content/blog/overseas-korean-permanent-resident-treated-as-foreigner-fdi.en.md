In some cases, yes. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act defines a "foreigner" as an individual holding a foreign nationality, a corporation established under foreign law and similar entities. However, for individuals who hold Korean nationality and reside permanently abroad, and who fall within the scope set by Presidential Decree, the Act also applies its provisions on foreigners. That scope is divided by permanent residency in the country of residence and the length of the stay permit.

This guide is for people who keep Korean nationality while living abroad and want to invest in a Korean company, and for Korean companies welcoming such shareholders. Because the investor is a Korean national, the foreign investment procedure is sometimes not considered at all, so it is worth checking eligibility first.

## Key Summary

- A "foreigner" under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act is an individual with a foreign nationality, a foreign corporation, or an international economic cooperation organization designated by Presidential Decree
- For Korean nationals residing permanently abroad who fall within the Presidential Decree, the Act's foreigner provisions also apply
- Covered persons: those who have obtained permanent residency in their country of residence / in a country without a permanent residency system, those who have received a stay permit of 4 years or more / in a country without a permanent residency system that grants only stay permits of less than 4 years, those who have lived there for 4 years or more and received a stay permit of 1 year or more
- A foreign investment requires an investment amount of 100 million won or more plus ownership of 10/100 or more of the voting shares or total capital contribution, or the dispatch or appointment of an officer
- A foreign investment is reported in advance to the Minister of Trade and Industry, and once the contribution has been paid in, the company is registered as a foreign-invested company
- The specific documents proving eligibility need to be confirmed case by case

## 1. Who is a "foreigner" under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act?

The Act defines a "foreigner" as an individual holding a foreign nationality, a corporation established under foreign law, and an international economic cooperation organization designated by Presidential Decree. A "Korean national" is an individual holding Korean nationality.

So an individual with Korean nationality only is, in principle, not a foreigner under this Act. However, the Act separately provides that its provisions on foreigners "also apply" to individuals who hold Korean nationality and reside permanently abroad, as set by Presidential Decree. The nationality does not change; it means the foreigner provisions also apply when the Foreign Investment Promotion Act is applied.

## 2. Who qualifies? Three criteria based on permanent residency and stay permits

The Enforcement Decree defines an individual residing permanently abroad as a person who falls under any of the following.

| Category | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Country with a permanent residency system | A person who has obtained permanent residency in the country of residence |
| Country without a permanent residency system | A person who has received a stay permit of 4 years or more |
| Country without a permanent residency system that grants only stay permits of less than 4 years | A person who has lived there for 4 years or more and has received a stay permit of 1 year or more |

If you live in a country that has a permanent residency system but hold only a fixed-term status for work or study, you do not directly meet the first criterion. Because the criteria depend on whether your country of residence has a permanent residency system and how many years your stay permit covers, check your residence documents from that country first.

## 3. What changes when the foreigner provisions also apply?

A covered person can invest by following the procedures the Act sets for foreigners. The main ones are:

- **Foreign investment report**: A foreigner who intends to make a foreign investment must report it in advance to the Minister of Trade and Industry as prescribed by Ordinance of the Ministry of Trade and Industry, and the Minister issues a report certificate without delay upon receiving the report.
- **Foreign-invested company registration**: Registration as a foreign-invested company is required when, for example, the payment of the contribution has been completed or the acquisition of shares has been completed.
- **Receiving bodies**: Receiving foreign investment reports and change reports, issuing report certificates, and registering foreign-invested companies and changes are entrusted to the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and the heads of foreign exchange banks.

Becoming a foreign-invested company allows you to consider the support programs set by the Act. Each program has its own requirements, however, so the application of the foreigner provisions does not automatically bring every benefit.

## 4. How large must the investment be to count as a foreign investment?

A foreign investment by share acquisition requires an investment amount of 100 million won or more and must fall under one of the following:

- Owning 10/100 or more of the total voting shares or total capital contribution issued by a Korean corporation or company (including a corporation being established)
- Owning shares of a Korean corporation or company while dispatching or appointing an officer to it

When two or more foreigners invest together, the investment amount is assessed per person. A Korean national who meets the permanent residency criteria must also meet these investment requirements to be recognized as making a foreign investment.

## 5. How is the foreign investment ratio counted for state and public property support?

The minimum foreign investment ratio used for leasing state and public property and similar support is a company in which foreign investors own 30/100 or more of the voting shares or total capital contribution. When calculating this ratio, the ownership ratio computed under a set method is not counted for foreign corporations owned directly or indirectly by Korean nationals or Korean corporations; the text states that "Korean nationals" here excludes individuals residing permanently abroad who fall under the Enforcement Decree.

If a Korean national residing permanently abroad invests through a foreign corporation, this calculation can affect the result, so the specific computation needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 6. What documents show that you qualify?

The Act and the Enforcement Decree set the scope of covered persons, but they do not separately specify which documents should show the permanent residency or the length of the stay permit when reporting. What to submit, such as a copy of the permanent residency card or stay permit documents, and in what form (including translation and authentication) needs to be confirmed case by case.

There is also a criminal penalty provision for anyone who submits false documents in connection with a permit or report under this Act. Requirements involving numbers, such as the length of a stay permit, should be matched precisely to the dates on the original documents.

## 7. Common problems

- Assuming you qualify while holding only a fixed-term stay permit in a country that has a permanent residency system
- Not checking that the stay permit period falls short of 4 years in a country without a permanent residency system
- Not separately checking the 100 million won investment amount or the 10/100 shareholding requirement
- Remitting funds first without a report and trying to treat it as a foreign investment later
- Assuming that once the foreigner provisions apply, you are treated as a foreigner under other laws such as foreign exchange, tax or immigration (treatment under other laws needs to be confirmed case by case)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I file a foreign investment report with Korean nationality only?

Individuals who hold Korean nationality, reside permanently abroad and meet the Enforcement Decree criteria are also covered by the Act's foreigner provisions, so they can consider the foreign investment procedure.

### Am I excluded if I do not have permanent residency?

In a country without a permanent residency system, a person with a stay permit of 4 years or more qualifies; in a country without a permanent residency system that grants only stay permits of less than 4 years, a person who has lived there for 4 years or more and received a stay permit of 1 year or more also qualifies.

### How much must I invest?

A foreign investment by share acquisition requires an investment amount of 100 million won or more plus ownership of 10/100 or more of the voting shares or total capital contribution, or the dispatch or appointment of an officer.

### Where is the report filed?

A foreign investment report is to be filed in advance with the Minister of Trade and Industry, and receiving it and issuing the report certificate are entrusted to the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and the heads of foreign exchange banks.

### Which documents prove that I qualify?

The type and form of supporting documents need to be confirmed case by case. It is advisable to prepare them in advance based on the permanent residency or stay permit documents issued by your country of residence.
