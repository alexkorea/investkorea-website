Not every foreign investment is reviewed. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act does not restrict foreign investment in principle, but makes exceptions such as cases that hinder the maintenance of national security and public order. The Enforcement Decree defines "national security harm" screening for cases where a foreigner seeks to substantially acquire management control of an already established Korean company by acquiring its shares, and the investment relates to defense goods production, export-controlled items or technology with high potential for military diversion, national secrets, core national technology, national high-tech strategic technology and similar areas.

This guide is for foreign investors considering the acquisition of shares in Korean technology or manufacturing companies, and for staff on the seller side. Whether a deal is covered can be confirmed before reporting, and the review periods and post-decision measures are fixed, so it pays to know them when planning the transaction timeline.

## Key Summary

- Grounds on which foreign investment may be restricted: hindering national security and public order, harming public health or the environment, and violating Korean law
- National security harm screening applies only when both conditions are met: (1) substantially acquiring management control through shares of an already established Korean company and (2) relating to defense goods, items with military diversion potential, national secrets, international peace efforts, core national technology or national high-tech strategic technology
- Before reporting the investment, a foreigner can ask the competent minister or the Minister of Trade and Industry to confirm whether it is covered; notice is given within 30 days of the request (excluding time spent supplementing materials)
- Before deliberation, an expert committee pre-review takes up to 90 days (one 30-day extension possible), and the decision is made through the Foreign Investment Committee within 45 days after that report
- The investment can be allowed with conditions such as a separate sale of a specific business or compliance with security measures
- If national security harm is found, shares already acquired must be transferred within 6 months of the decision (extendable within 1 year with approval for unavoidable reasons)
- Once 3 years have passed since a "not covered" notice or an approval notice, the same investment cannot be reviewed again unless there is a material change in circumstances

## 1. Which foreign investments are screened for national security harm?

The Foreign Investment Promotion Act states that foreigners may make foreign investments without restriction unless a law provides otherwise, with the following exceptions:

- Cases that hinder the maintenance of national security and public order
- Cases that harm public health and sanitation or environmental conservation, or are markedly contrary to good morals
- Cases that violate Korean laws

The Enforcement Decree turns the national security part of these exceptions into "national security harm." A deal is screened only if it meets both of the following conditions.

| Condition | Details |
|---|---|
| (1) Deal type | A foreigner seeks to substantially acquire management control of an already established Korean company by acquiring its shares or equity |
| (2) Related area (one or more) | Risk of disrupting defense goods production / export-controlled items or technology with high potential for military use / risk of disclosing contracts handled as national secrets / risk of seriously hindering international efforts for peace and security / high likelihood of leaking core national technology / high likelihood of leaking national high-tech strategic technology |

An investment that sets up a new corporation therefore does not fit deal type (1). The issue mainly arises in acquisitions where shares of an existing company are bought to gain management control. Investment in a company that operates a defense industry business has a separate procedure requiring prior permission.

## 2. Can I confirm whether my deal is covered before reporting?

Yes. Before filing the foreign investment report, a foreigner can ask the competent minister or the Minister of Trade and Industry to confirm, with the items set by the ministry's public notice, whether the investment meets both conditions above. A competent minister who receives a request forwards it to the Minister of Trade and Industry without delay.

The Minister of Trade and Industry must notify the foreigner of the result within 30 days from the date of the request. If supplementary materials are requested, the time taken to supplement them is not counted in the 30 days. The specific items needed for the request follow the public notice, so they need to be confirmed case by case.

## 3. When is a review opened?

The Minister of Trade and Industry must decide whether national security harm exists, after deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee, in any of the following cases:

- The foreigner reports that the investment meets both conditions
- The competent minister or the Director of the National Intelligence Service requests a review
- The Minister of Trade and Industry finds it necessary

In other words, a review can be opened even if the investor does not report it, at the request of relevant agencies or at the minister's own judgment. For the confirmation and review, the minister may ask heads of relevant agencies and the National Intelligence Service for investigation results or materials, and may ask the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and others to confirm facts.

## 4. In what order does the review proceed, and how long does it take?

| Stage | Period |
|---|---|
| Prior confirmation before reporting | Notice within 30 days of the request (excluding supplementation time) |
| Expert committee pre-review | Report to the Foreign Investment Committee within 90 days of the request (one extension of up to 30 days for unavoidable reasons) |
| Foreign Investment Committee deliberation and decision | Within 45 days of the pre-review report |
| Notice of result | Without delay after the decision |

The expert committee sits under the Foreign Investment Committee to review national security harm efficiently. It has up to 20 members including the chair, drawn from senior officials of relevant ministries, staff of the National Intelligence Service, the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and the Korea Trade Security Agency, and experts in defense, industrial technology, supply chains, security and law.

Where the investment has already been approved after deliberation under another law and relates to leakage of core national technology or national high-tech strategic technology, the review and decision procedure can be skipped.

## 5. What happens after the decision? Conditional approval and share transfer

After the decision, the Minister of Trade and Industry gives notice without delay allowing or disallowing the share acquisition, stating whether national security harm exists, the reasons, and any conditions. If deemed necessary, the investment can be allowed with conditions, such as a separate sale of a specific business or compliance with security measures, following the committee's deliberation.

| Decision | Obligation of a foreigner who has already acquired shares |
|---|---|
| National security harm found | Transfer within 6 months of the decision to a Korean national, Korean corporation or foreigner posing no national security concern |
| Condition breached after conditional approval | Transfer within 6 months from when the minister learns of the breach |
| Unavoidable reasons | Transfer period extendable within 1 year with the minister's approval |

Once 3 years have passed since a notice that the investment is not covered or a notice allowing it, the same investment cannot be reviewed and decided again, unless a material change in circumstances afterwards makes a new review necessary.

## 6. How should the deal timeline be planned? Common problems

If the target company may relate to the areas above, it is safer to build the confirmation and review periods into the contract timeline. Common problems include:

- Scheduling without distinguishing a new-company investment from an acquisition of an existing company
- Not checking whether the target's technology qualifies as core national technology or similar
- Assuming there is no review unless the investor reports one (it can also be opened at agencies' request or the minister's judgment)
- Not managing conditions of a conditional approval (separate sale, security compliance) after the deal
- Treating the prior permission procedure for defense industry investment as the same as this review

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an investment that sets up a new corporation also subject to national security review?

The Enforcement Decree screens cases where a foreigner seeks to substantially acquire management control of an already established Korean company through its shares, and a related-area condition such as defense goods or core national technology must also be met.

### Can I find out in advance whether my investment is covered?

You can request confirmation from the competent minister or the Minister of Trade and Industry before filing the foreign investment report, and you will be notified within 30 days of the request. Time spent supplementing materials is not counted.

### How long does the review take?

The expert committee pre-review takes up to 90 days (one extension of up to 30 days), and the decision is made through the Foreign Investment Committee within 45 days of that report.

### What happens to shares already purchased if harm is found?

They must be transferred within 6 months of the decision to a Korean national, Korean corporation or foreigner posing no national security concern, and the period can be extended within 1 year with approval for unavoidable reasons.

### Can a review be reopened after an approval notice?

Once 3 years have passed since the notice, the investment cannot be reviewed again unless a material change in circumstances makes a new review necessary.
