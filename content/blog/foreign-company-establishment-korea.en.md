Setting up a corporation is the most common way for foreigners to operate a business in Korea. The stock company (주식회사, Co., Ltd.) structure is preferred for investment credibility and fundraising.

## Legal Framework

Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, foreigners who invest KRW 100 million or more in a domestic corporation qualify as **foreign-invested enterprises**, which may be eligible for tax incentives.

## Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1: Company Name Check**
The same company name cannot be used in the same industry within the same metropolitan/provincial area. Check availability at the Supreme Court's online registry.

**Step 2: Foreign Investment Notification (KOTRA or Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank)**
Required for investments of KRW 100M+. You receive a foreign investment notification certificate after filing.

**Step 3: Capital Transfer**
Transfer capital to a designated Korean bank account and obtain a bank balance certificate.

**Step 4: Draft and Notarize Articles of Incorporation**
Articles must be notarized by a Korean notary. Foreign-language documents require Korean translation and notarization.

**Step 5: Corporate Registration**
File for registration at the competent District Court registry. Processing takes approximately one week.

**Step 6: Business License Registration**
After registration, apply for a business license at the local tax office. Operations can begin after the license is issued.

## Required Documents (Foreign Investor)

- Passport copy (notarized)
- Foreign investment notification certificate
- Bank balance certificate
- Notarized articles of incorporation
- Director acceptance letter
- Signature notarization (if no Korean seal)

## Minimum Capital

There is no statutory minimum capital for incorporation itself. However, KRW 100 million or more is required for foreign-invested enterprise status.
