## Procedure and Key Considerations for Foreign Company Real Estate Purchase in Korea 2026

Foreign companies acquiring real estate in Korea must comply with **Bank of Korea reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act**, **acquisition reporting under the Foreigner's Land Acquisition Act**, acquisition tax payment, and registration procedures. Acquiring housing in regulated areas triggers a 12% heavy acquisition tax rate, and purchases in transaction-permit zones require separate government approval.

This guide covers the 5-step acquisition procedure, key points under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), rental business eligibility, and tax reporting obligations — all based on 2026 standards.

## 1. Mandatory Reporting Obligations for Foreign Companies

Foreign companies acquiring Korean real estate have two separate reporting obligations.

### (1) Foreign Exchange Transaction Act Report (Bank of Korea / Exchange Bank)

When a non-resident foreign company brings funds into Korea or acquires Korean real estate, **advance reporting** is required under Article 18 of the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act.

- Reporting authority: Bank of Korea or authorized foreign exchange bank (primary transaction bank)
- Timing: Before executing the acquisition contract or before remitting funds (advance reporting is the standard)
- Penalty for non-reporting: A percentage of the acquisition price (subject to variation depending on circumstances)

### (2) Foreigner's Land Acquisition Act Report

When a foreign company acquires land (including buildings) in Korea, it must report to the local district office **within 60 days** of acquisition.

- Reporting authority: City/county/district office where the property is located
- Scope: Applies to foreign corporations and domestic companies where foreign individuals or entities hold a controlling stake
- Properties in designated permit zones require prior government approval — these are distinct from general areas where post-contract reporting is sufficient.

> **Key point:** Both reports are separate requirements. Failing to complete either can result in legal penalties.

## 2. 5-Step Real Estate Acquisition Procedure

Foreign companies must follow these five steps in order when acquiring Korean real estate.

### Step 1: Preliminary Review and Advisory

- Confirm whether the property is in a regulated zone (transaction-permit zone, military protection area, cultural heritage zone, etc.)
- Check whether the area is a government-designated regulated area (heavy tax rates apply for housing in such areas)
- Review FIPA applicability (registered foreign investment companies may qualify for incentives)
- Engage qualified professionals (registered administrative agent, tax accountant, attorney)

### Step 2: Foreign Exchange Reporting and Fund Transfer

- File advance report under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act (Bank of Korea or exchange bank)
- Transfer investment funds to a designated domestic account
- Prepare documentation of fund source and transfer route (required to meet anti-money laundering standards)

### Step 3: Purchase Contract and Deposit Payment

- Execute the real estate purchase agreement (Korean-language original; translation recommended)
- Pay the deposit (typically 10% of the purchase price)
- Review special clauses (e.g., foreign exchange report confirmation attached as condition)

### Step 4: Foreigner's Land Acquisition Report or Permit

- General areas: Report can be filed after contract execution and before final payment
- Transaction-permit zones: Must obtain **prior approval** from the district office before final payment (contracts without approval are void)

### Step 5: Final Payment, Acquisition Tax, and Registration

- Pay the balance and file acquisition tax within 60 days of acquisition date
- Apply for property ownership transfer registration at the district registry office
- File the Foreigner's Land Acquisition report (within 60 days of acquisition, for report-type areas)

## 3. Key Considerations Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Foreign companies registered as foreign-invested enterprises under FIPA may qualify for various tax incentives and support programs. However, several important limitations apply to real estate acquisition.

### Key FIPA Rules for Real Estate

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum investment for FIPA recognition | KRW 100 million or more (subject to change) |
| Eligible property uses | Directly related to the company's business purpose |
| Restrictions on pure property investment | Rental income as sole purpose generally does not qualify for FIPA incentives |
| Tax incentives | Companies in designated foreign investment zones may receive acquisition tax and property tax reductions |

### Key Warnings

1. **Business use vs. investment use:** Acquiring property for offices, factories, or warehouses for business operations is generally unrestricted. Acquisition solely for rental income or capital appreciation may be excluded from FIPA benefits.
2. **Check foreign investment zone designation:** Companies meeting certain requirements can apply for designation as a foreign investment zone, which provides land subsidies and tax reductions.
3. **Tax incentive application deadlines:** FIPA tax benefit applications have strict deadlines and eligibility criteria — confirm in advance.

## 4. Land Transaction Permit Zone Regulations

The government designates certain areas as **Land Transaction Permit Zones** to curb speculation. Acquiring land (including buildings) above a certain area within these zones requires **prior approval** from the district office.

### General Permit Threshold Areas (Varies by Zone)

| Land Use | Permit Required Above |
|---|---|
| Residential zone | 18 ㎡ |
| Commercial zone | 20 ㎡ |
| Industrial zone | 660 ㎡ |
| Green belt, farmland, forest | 100–500 ㎡ |

> Exact thresholds vary by zone and government notice. Always confirm with the local district office.

### Permit Requirements

- **Genuine use principle:** The acquisition purpose must demonstrate actual use (residence or business) — speculative purchases are denied.
- **Mandatory use period:** Permit recipients must use the property as declared for a set period (typically 2–5 years).
- **Transactions without permit:** The contract is void, and performance penalties may be imposed.

Foreign companies face the same permit process as domestic companies but must simultaneously handle foreign exchange reporting and Foreigner's Land Acquisition Act reporting — making the overall process significantly more complex. Professional assistance is strongly recommended.

## 5. Acquisition Tax and Tax Reporting for Foreign Companies

### Acquisition Tax Rates

| Property Type | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Housing (outside regulated area, general acquisition) | 4% (corporate basis) |
| Housing (regulated area or heavy-tax trigger) | 12% |
| Commercial real estate / land | 4% |
| Factory / warehouse (industrial use) | 4% |

In practice, corporate acquisition of housing is frequently subject to the 12% rate regardless of regulated area status. Confirm the exact applicable rate with a tax advisor before acquisition.

### Tax Reporting Procedure

1. **Acquisition tax:** Report and pay within 60 days of acquisition at the local district office
2. **VAT:** If acquiring business-use property, register as a taxable business and confirm input tax credit eligibility
3. **Property tax:** Assessed annually based on ownership as of June 1; paid in installments in July and September
4. **Corporate income tax:** Rental income and capital gains from real estate are subject to corporate income tax

### Taxes on Disposition (Corporate)

When a foreign company sells Korean real estate, any capital gain is subject to **corporate income tax** — combined with the company's total taxable income, not taxed separately as a capital gain. However, additional taxes may apply on housing disposals.

## 6. Rental Business: Eligibility and Business Registration

Foreign companies operating a rental business in Korea must complete **domestic business registration**.

### Rental Business Eligibility

| Rental Type | Permitted? |
|---|---|
| Commercial building rental (offices / retail) | Generally permitted |
| Residential housing rental | Generally permitted; may be excluded from FIPA incentives |
| Short-term rental (Airbnb-type) | Requires separate Tourism Promotion Act permit |
| Farmland rental | Generally restricted (check Agricultural Land Act) |

### Business Registration Procedure

1. Secure a domestic business address
2. Apply for business registration at the local tax office (including corporate registry and foreign company documentation)
3. File VAT and corporate income tax returns when rental income is generated

> **Warning:** A foreign company collecting rental income in Korea without a business registration may be treated as a tax evader, and the tenant may have withholding tax obligations. Both landlord and tenant should review their tax risk position.

## 7. Common Problems and Prevention Checklist

### Frequent Issues for Foreign Companies

1. **Remitting funds without prior Bank of Korea report:** Violates the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act; fines apply.
2. **Executing a contract in a transaction-permit zone without prior approval:** Contract is void; disputes over return of purchase price arise.
3. **Missing the acquisition tax filing deadline:** 20% non-filing penalty.
4. **Omitting the Foreigner's Land Acquisition report:** Fines and registration delays.
5. **Collecting rental income without business registration:** Underreporting of corporate income tax and VAT leads to back-tax assessments.

### Pre/During/Post-Acquisition Checklist

**Before Acquisition**
- [ ] Confirm property's regulatory status (transaction-permit zone, regulated area, etc.)
- [ ] Verify foreign exchange reporting requirements
- [ ] Pre-assess acquisition tax and corporate tax rates
- [ ] Review FIPA incentive eligibility

**During Acquisition**
- [ ] Obtain confirmation of completed foreign exchange report
- [ ] Secure district approval if property is in a transaction-permit zone
- [ ] Review special clauses in the purchase contract

**After Acquisition**
- [ ] File and pay acquisition tax within 60 days of acquisition
- [ ] Submit Foreigner's Land Acquisition report (within 60 days)
- [ ] Register business and execute lease agreement (if running rental operations)
- [ ] Retain all documentation after registration is complete

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can a foreign company purchase an apartment in Korea?**

A: It is legally possible, but corporate acquisition of housing is subject to heavy tax rates (up to 12%), and the transaction may not qualify for FIPA incentives. If the intended use is residential rental, additional regulatory requirements apply — consult a professional before proceeding.

**Q2. What happens if a contract is executed in a transaction-permit zone without prior government approval?**

A: The contract is legally void. Even if the full purchase price is paid, ownership cannot be transferred, and performance penalties may be imposed. Always confirm zone status before signing any contract.

**Q3. Does a foreign company have to register a business to rent out commercial property in Korea?**

A: Yes. Business registration is mandatory when generating business income in Korea. Collecting rental income without registration may result in back-tax assessments for both VAT and corporate income tax.

**Q4. Does FIPA registration automatically reduce acquisition tax?**

A: Not automatically. Companies in formally designated foreign investment zones may receive acquisition tax and property tax reductions, but standard FIPA registration alone does not trigger the exemption. Specific eligibility criteria and application procedures apply.

**Q5. If a foreign company uses a Korean subsidiary to buy property, does the same regulation apply?**

A: A domestic Korean company in which a foreign individual or foreign company holds a significant equity stake may be treated equivalently to a foreign company for regulatory purposes. Always have a legal professional review the corporate structure before proceeding.
