When a foreign-invested company uses its unappropriated retained earnings for set purposes, such as building or expanding its own plant, that use is treated as foreign investment. In that case the foreign-invested company itself is treated as a foreign national under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the foreign investment amount is the amount used multiplied by the foreign investment ratio. Because it is a method of foreign investment, the general rule of reporting in advance applies.

This guide is for finance and administration managers of profitable foreign-invested companies in Korea and for head-office staff overseas. If you plan to put profits back into Korean facilities instead of paying them out as dividends, first distinguish three forms of "reinvestment" with different reporting timing: using unappropriated retained earnings, capitalizing reserves, and contributing dividends and other proceeds.

## Key Summary

- A foreign-invested company using unappropriated retained earnings for set purposes is foreign investment
- Qualifying uses: (1) building or expanding a plant (a business site for non-manufacturing businesses) or research facility; (2) purchasing capital goods or research equipment needed for the company's business
- In that case the foreign-invested company is treated as a foreign national under the Act
- Foreign investment amount = amount used × foreign investment ratio (the ratio of shares or equity held by foreign investors to the company's total shares or equity)
- As a rule, foreign investment is reported to the Minister of Trade and Industry in advance, and using unappropriated retained earnings is not on the list of cases that may be reported within 60 days after acquiring shares
- When a foreign investor acquires shares issued by capitalizing reserves, revaluation reserves and the like, or acquires shares by contributing proceeds from shares it acquired, the report may be made within 60 days after acquiring the shares, and change registration is required
- Investment funds must not be used for purposes other than those reported

## 1. Why does using unappropriated retained earnings count as foreign investment?

The Foreign Investment Promotion Act lists among the methods of foreign investment "a foreign-invested company using unappropriated retained earnings for set purposes such as building or expanding its plant". Even without bringing in new money from abroad, putting profits earned in Korea back into Korean facilities is recognized as foreign investment.

In this case the foreign-invested company is treated as a foreign national under the Act. This can be understood to mean that the company itself goes through procedures that apply to foreign nationals, such as reporting; how the reporting party is shown and which forms are used need to be checked case by case.

## 2. Which uses qualify?

There are two qualifying uses.

| Use | Details |
|---|---|
| Building or expanding facilities | Building or expanding a plant (for a business other than manufacturing under the Korean Standard Industrial Classification, a business site) or research facility |
| Capital goods and research equipment | Purchasing capital goods or research equipment needed for the company's business |

A non-manufacturing company qualifies if it builds or expands a business site. By contrast, uses not on the list, such as working capital or repaying borrowings, are hard to treat as falling under this method. Whether a particular expense falls within the uses above needs to be checked case by case.

## 3. How is the foreign investment amount calculated?

The foreign investment amount is the amount used multiplied by the foreign investment ratio. The foreign investment ratio means the ratio of shares or equity held by foreign investors to the foreign-invested company's shares or equity.

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Amount used | The part of unappropriated retained earnings used for a qualifying purpose |
| Foreign investment ratio | The ratio of the company's shares or equity held by foreign investors |
| Foreign investment amount | Amount used × foreign investment ratio |

So in a joint venture where foreign investors do not hold all the shares, only the foreign share, not the full amount of retained earnings used, counts as the foreign investment amount. The reference date for the ratio used in the calculation needs to be checked case by case.

## 4. When and how is it reported?

As a rule, a foreign national who intends to make foreign investment by one of the listed methods must report it to the Minister of Trade and Industry in advance. The exceptions that may be reported within 60 days after acquiring shares (acquiring existing shares of listed companies, capitalizing reserves, mergers and the like, contributing proceeds, converting convertible bonds and the like) do not include using unappropriated retained earnings, so it is safer to schedule the report before spending on the facilities.

- On receiving a report, the Minister of Trade and Industry issues a report certificate to the reporting party without delay
- Receiving reports and change reports and issuing report certificates are delegated to the head of KOTRA and the heads of foreign exchange banks
- If set items in the report, such as the foreign investment ratio, change, a report reflecting the change may be made

The usage plan to state in the report and the documents to attach, such as financial statements or board resolutions, need to be checked case by case.

## 5. How does it differ from capitalizing reserves or contributing proceeds?

There are several ways to put profits back into the company, and the reporting timing differs.

| Method | Why it is foreign investment | Reporting timing |
|---|---|---|
| Using unappropriated retained earnings for facilities, capital goods and the like | The company is treated as a foreign national; amount used × foreign investment ratio | In advance, as the general rule |
| A foreign investor acquires shares issued by capitalizing reserves, revaluation reserves or reserves under other laws | Acquisition of shares | May be reported within 60 days after acquiring the shares |
| A foreign investor acquires shares by contributing proceeds (dividends and the like) from shares acquired under the Act | Acquisition of shares | May be reported within 60 days after acquiring the shares |

If the report for capitalized reserves or contributed proceeds was made within 60 days, the foreign-invested company registration must also be changed. In addition, a foreign investor owning shares because the foreign-invested company capitalized its earned surplus reserve is included in the investment amount (the amount of shares acquired).

## 6. Registration and follow-up

The listed reasons for foreign-invested company registration are completing payment of the investment, completing the acquisition of shares, and completing a contribution to a nonprofit corporation. Using unappropriated retained earnings is not separately listed, so how to reflect it in the registration after use needs to be checked case by case. If set items such as the foreign investment ratio or the company's trade name change, change registration must be made.

Foreign investors and foreign-invested companies must not use investment funds for purposes other than those reported. Keep the use stated in the report and the actual spending consistent, so that money reported for a plant expansion is not spent elsewhere.

## 7. Common mistakes

- Using retained earnings for non-qualifying purposes such as working capital or repaying borrowings and trying to report it as foreign investment
- In a joint venture, counting the full amount used as the foreign investment amount
- Assuming the 60-day subsequent report applies, as with capitalized reserves, and trying to report after spending on the facilities
- The reported use and the actual spending diverging
- Forgetting change registration after reporting capitalized reserves or contributed proceeds

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can it be foreign investment without bringing money in from abroad?

Yes. A foreign-invested company using unappropriated retained earnings to build or expand plants or research facilities, or to purchase capital goods or research equipment needed for its business, is treated as making foreign investment.

### Is the foreign investment amount the full amount used?

No. It is the amount used multiplied by the foreign investment ratio (the ratio of shares or equity held by foreign investors).

### Can we report within 60 days after using the funds?

Using unappropriated retained earnings is not on the list of cases that may be reported within 60 days after acquiring shares. It is safer to plan on reporting in advance, as the general rule requires.

### Does a service company qualify?

For a business other than manufacturing, building or expanding a business site qualifies in place of a plant. Purchasing capital goods or research equipment is also a qualifying use.

### How is this different from turning reserves into capital?

When a foreign investor acquires shares issued by capitalizing reserves, revaluation reserves and the like, the report may be made within 60 days after acquiring the shares, and change registration is required. Using unappropriated retained earnings for facilities and the like is a separate method of foreign investment.
