## Overview of Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in Korea

Foreign Direct Investment in Korea is governed by the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), Article 5, which mandates prior notification for all qualifying investment activities. This includes acquiring shares or equity interests in Korean companies, providing long-term loans, and establishing new corporations in Korea.

## FDI Reporting Agencies

Under Article 5 of the FIPA, foreign investors can report their investment through:

1. **Designated Foreign Exchange Banks**: Most major Korean commercial banks accept FDI notifications (most common route)
2. **KOTRA Investment Centers**: Located in Seoul and major cities
3. **Foreign Investment Support Centers**: Under the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE)

### Online Registration
- Online notification is available through the Invest KOREA portal (www.investkorea.org)
- Requires digital certificate or foreign registration card-based authentication

## Step-by-Step FDI Registration Procedure

### Step 1: Investment Planning
- Determine investment method (new share issuance, acquisition of existing shares, long-term loan, etc.)
- Minimum investment amount: **KRW 100 million** (approximately USD 75,000) as required by Article 2 of FIPA
- Verify whether the target industry is open to foreign investment (restricted and prohibited sectors exist)

### Step 2: Submit FDI Notification
Required documents:
- **Foreign Investment Notification Form** (Form No. 1)
- Investor identification documents (passport or corporate registration documents)
- Share/equity acquisition agreement (if applicable)
- Proof of investment fund source
- Articles of incorporation (for new corporation establishment)

### Step 3: Transfer Investment Funds
- Transfer investment capital through a designated foreign exchange bank after completing the notification
- The transferred amount must match the notified investment details
- Report the sale of foreign exchange instruments under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

### Step 4: Register as Foreign-Invested Enterprise
- After completing share acquisition or corporation establishment, apply for **Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration**
- Apply through MOTIE or KOTRA
- Obtain the Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration Certificate (required for D-8 visa applications)

### Step 5: Post-Investment Management
- Report changes in investment status as they occur
- Notify foreign exchange bank when remitting dividends, interest, or other investment returns
- File liquidation report when winding down investment

## Industry Restrictions for Foreign Investment

Certain industries have restrictions or prohibitions on foreign investment under FIPA and related regulations:
- **Restricted sectors**: Air transport, coastal shipping, broadcasting (partial foreign ownership allowed up to certain thresholds)
- **Prohibited sectors**: Defense/national security industries, nuclear energy
- Always verify with KOTRA's foreign investment guide or MOTIE before selecting an investment target

## Tax Incentives

Foreign investors making qualifying FDI under FIPA may be eligible for:
- Corporate/income tax reductions under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act (for high-technology projects)
- Acquisition tax and property tax exemptions (under local government ordinances)
- Incentives vary by investment amount, industry, and region — confirm in advance
