Foreigners can freely acquire most real estate in Korea, but must complete a **property acquisition report** after purchase. In certain restricted areas, a prior permit is required.

## Legal Framework

- **Foreigner's Land Act**: Governs reporting or permit requirements for land acquisition
- **Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act**: Requires reporting within 30 days of contract

## Report vs. Permit

| Zone | Requirement |
|---|---|
| General residential/commercial | Report only (within 60 days of acquisition) |
| Military base proximity zones | Prior permit required |
| Natural environment conservation zones | Prior permit required |

Most apartments and offices in urban areas require only the post-acquisition report.

## Acquisition Process

```
Step 1: Sign purchase contract
        ↓
Step 2: Real estate transaction report (within 30 days — by agent or directly)
        ↓
Step 3: Pay balance, transfer ownership registration
        ↓
Step 4: Foreigner land acquisition report (within 60 days of registration — submit to local office)
```

## Documents Required for Report

1. Foreigner land acquisition report form
2. Purchase contract copy
3. Passport or alien registration card copy
4. Property registry extract
5. Source of funds documentation (overseas remittance certificate, etc.)

## Capital Inflow

When bringing purchase funds from abroad, transfer through an authorized **foreign exchange bank** and complete foreign currency inflow reporting. Authorities are increasingly scrutinizing the source of funds.

## Taxes After Acquisition

Foreigners are taxed the same as Korean nationals:
- **Acquisition tax**: 1.1–13.4% of purchase price (varies by number of homes owned)
- **Property tax**: Assessed annually in July and September
- **Comprehensive Real Estate Tax**: Applied if total assessed value exceeds threshold
