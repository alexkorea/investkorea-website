Yes, it can. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act provides that land, factories and other property owned by the State, local governments, public institutions and local public enterprises may be sold to foreign-invested companies and similar operators by private contract, notwithstanding the relevant provisions of the State Property Act and other laws. However, there is a foreign investment ratio requirement for companies that may buy, the ratio must be maintained for a set period after the contract, and the sale contract may be terminated or cancelled if the requirements are not kept.

This guide is for foreign-invested companies looking to secure a factory or business site in Korea, and for investors considering purchase rather than lease. Leasing and rent reduction are covered in a separate post, so here we focus on purchase (sale) and contract cancellation.

## Key Summary

- Land, factories and other property owned by the State, local governments, public institutions and local public enterprises ("the State, etc.") can be sold to foreign-invested companies and similar operators by private contract
- Foreign-invested companies that may buy: companies in which foreign investors own 30/100 or more of the total voting shares or total capital contribution
- The minimum foreign investment ratio must be maintained for 5 years from the date of the private contract
- Exceptions to the ratio requirement exist where the contribution to the national economy, such as job creation or investment size, is large
- If paying in one lump sum is difficult, for State-owned land the payment date can be deferred within 1 year or payment made in installments within 20 years, with interest not exceeding 4 percent per year
- The contract may be terminated or cancelled on 6 grounds: unpaid purchase price, false contract, failure to start the business, failure to perform promised hiring or investment, failure to maintain the ratio, and termination by agreement
- A special cancellation registration is made at sale, and the State, etc. takes measures to restore rights upon termination or cancellation

## 1. Which property can be sold by private contract, and by whom?

The property covered is land, factories or other property owned by the State, local governments, public institutions or local public enterprises (excluding local government-directly-managed enterprises). The Act calls these owners "the State, etc." and the property "land, etc."

Notwithstanding the relevant provisions of the State Property Act, the Public Property and Commodity Management Act, the Act on the Management of Public Institutions and other laws, the State, etc. may sell its land, etc. by private contract to foreign-invested companies or operators of foreign investment environment improvement facilities. Because the provision says "may", the owning body decides whether to sell a particular site and on what terms.

## 2. Which foreign-invested companies may buy?

Foreign-invested companies that may buy land, etc. are limited to those meeting the minimum foreign investment ratio. The Enforcement Decree defines this as a company in which foreign investors own 30/100 or more of the total voting shares or total capital contribution of the foreign-invested company.

When calculating the ratio, for foreign corporations owned directly or indirectly by Korean nationals (excluding nationals residing permanently abroad who fall under the Enforcement Decree) or Korean corporations, the ownership ratio computed under a set method is not counted. If you invest through a foreign corporation, calculate the recognized ratio in advance.

## 3. How long must the ratio be maintained? Are there exceptions?

The buying company must maintain the minimum foreign investment ratio for 5 years from the date of the private contract. Temporary failure to maintain it within a period set by Ordinance of the Ministry of Trade and Industry is excluded.

However, the ratio requirement does not apply to a foreign-invested company with a large contribution to the national economy, in terms of job creation, foreign investment amount, technology transfer and so on, that falls under one of the following:

- A foreign investment report stating that new jobs exceeding the number of regular employees set by Presidential Decree will be created within 3 years
- A foreign investment report stating that an investment of at least the amount set by Presidential Decree will be made within 5 years
- A decision granting tax reduction or exemption under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act
- A business making a considerable contribution to expanding social overhead capital, adjusting the industrial structure or the fiscal independence of local governments, which the Minister of Trade and Industry finds necessary after deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee

The number of regular employees and the investment amount used for these exceptions follow separate criteria in the Enforcement Decree, so the specific figures need to be confirmed case by case. If you bought under an exception, keeping the reported hiring and investment plan is directly linked to the cancellation grounds discussed below.

## 4. Can the purchase price be paid in installments?

Where it is recognized that the buyer has difficulty paying the purchase price in one lump sum, the payment date may be deferred or installment payment allowed. The methods set by the Enforcement Decree are:

| Owner | Method |
|---|---|
| Land, etc. owned by the State | Defer the payment date within 1 year, or pay in installments within 20 years |
| Land, etc. owned by a local government | Defer the payment date or pay in installments as set by local ordinance |

The interest applied in this case may not exceed 4 percent per year. The specific method for land, etc. owned by public institutions needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 5. When can the sale contract be terminated or cancelled? Six grounds

The State, etc. may terminate or cancel the sale contract if a foreign-invested company that bought land, etc. by private contract falls under any of the following:

1. The purchase price is in arrears
2. It is discovered that the contract was concluded by false statements, submission of false supporting documents or other improper means
3. The business is not started without special reason by the scheduled start date in the contract after the private contract
4. The requirements promised when buying under a ratio exception, such as job creation or investment, are not performed
5. The minimum foreign investment ratio is not maintained for the 5 years
6. Otherwise, the State, etc. and the foreign-invested company agree that termination or cancellation of the contract is necessary

For grounds 1 (unpaid price) and 3 (failure to start the business), the contract is not terminated or cancelled if the company complies within the period set by Ordinance of the Ministry of Trade and Industry after the State, etc. orders correction. If you receive a correction order, responding within the deadline matters above all.

## 6. What are the special registration and restoration of rights?

When the State, etc. sells land, etc. by private contract, it must make a special registration stating that the sale contract may be cancelled if any of grounds 2 to 6 above arises. Because this special clause is recorded in the buying company's property register, it may affect plans to create security or resell.

When terminating or cancelling the contract, the State, etc. must without delay take the measures necessary to restore rights to the land, etc. How facilities already built or price already paid are settled needs to be confirmed according to the individual contract and case.

## 7. How does purchase differ from lease? Common problems

The State, etc. may also lease the same land, etc. to foreign-invested companies by private contract, and companies eligible to lease must also meet the same minimum foreign investment ratio (30/100 or more) and maintain it for 5 years from the date of the private contract. Lease periods and rent reductions are governed by separate provisions.

Common problems with purchase:

- A capital increase that adds Korean shareholders pushes the foreign investment ratio below 30/100 within the 5 years
- The scheduled business start date in the contract is set earlier than is realistic
- An exception is granted on the basis of a hiring or investment plan, and the plan is not kept
- The price falls into arrears during installment payment and the correction order deadline is missed

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can any foreign-invested company buy State land by private contract?

The company must be one in which foreign investors own 30/100 or more of the total voting shares or total capital contribution, with exceptions where the contribution to the national economy is large. The owning body decides whether to sell.

### Can we sell shares after the purchase?

The minimum foreign investment ratio must be maintained for 5 years from the date of the private contract; failing to maintain it is a ground for terminating or cancelling the sale contract.

### Can the price be paid in installments?

If paying in one lump sum is recognized as difficult, for State-owned land, etc. the payment date can be deferred within 1 year or payment made in installments within 20 years, and the interest may not exceed 4 percent per year. Land owned by local governments follows local ordinance.

### Is the contract cancelled immediately if the business start is delayed?

It is not terminated or cancelled if the company complies within the period set by Ordinance of the Ministry of Trade and Industry after the State, etc. orders correction. The same applies to an unpaid price.

### Why is a special registration made?

It records that the sale contract may be cancelled if grounds such as a false contract, failure to start the business, failure to perform requirements, failure to maintain the ratio or termination by agreement arise.
