## Overview: Setting Up a Korean Corporation as a Foreigner

Foreign nationals wishing to establish a corporation in Korea are subject to dual regulatory requirements: the Commercial Act (Article 172 governing corporation establishment) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). FDI notification under FIPA must precede corporate registration under the Commercial Act.

## Pre-Establishment Preparation

### Choosing Your Business Structure
Types of legal entities available to foreign investors in Korea:
- **주식회사 (Corporation/Co., Ltd.)**: Most common; no minimum capital requirement after Commercial Act amendment
- **유한회사 (Limited Liability Company)**: Up to 50 members; simpler establishment process
- **합자회사 / 합명회사 (Partnership)**: Suitable for small-scale businesses

### Industry Selection
- Confirm whether your target industry is open to foreign investment
- Certain sectors (healthcare, broadcasting, education, etc.) have foreign ownership restrictions

## Step-by-Step Corporation Establishment Process

### Step 1: Foreign Investment Notification (Prerequisite)
- File a prior notification with a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA under Article 5 of FIPA
- Submit business plan and investor identification documents
- Minimum investment: **KRW 100 million (approx. USD 75,000)**

### Step 2: Open a Bank Account and Deposit Capital
- Open a temporary corporate account at a domestic bank
- Transfer investment funds from overseas (in compliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act)
- Obtain a capital deposit confirmation certificate

### Step 3: Draft and Notarize Articles of Incorporation
- Prepare articles of incorporation in Korean
- Notarize with a court-appointed notary or through a court notarization process
- Mandatory provisions include: business purpose, trade name, total authorized shares, par value per share, shares issued at incorporation, registered office location, and director/auditor information

### Step 4: Organizational Meeting and Board Formation
- Promoter-only establishment (발기설립) is the most common method
- Appoint at least one director (a single director is sufficient for small companies)
- Elect a representative director (CEO)

### Step 5: Apply for Corporate Registration
- File corporate registration at the local district court registry
- Required documents:
  - Corporate registration application form
  - Articles of incorporation
  - Share subscription certificates
  - Capital deposit confirmation certificate
  - Board meeting minutes
  - Directors'/auditors' acceptance of appointment
  - Passport or resident registration abstract (for directors/auditors)
- Registration license tax: 0.4% of registered capital (Seoul rate)

### Step 6: Register as Foreign-Invested Enterprise
- After completing corporate registration, apply for Foreign-Invested Enterprise registration
- Submit to KOTRA or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE)
- Obtain the Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration Certificate

### Step 7: Business Registration
- Apply for business registration at the local tax office
- Submit corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, and business plan
- Obtain business registration certificate

## Post-Establishment Obligations

After incorporation, the following steps must be completed:
- **4 Major Social Insurances**: National Health Insurance, National Pension, Employment Insurance, Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance
- **Employment Contracts**: Required for all employees
- **Corporate Bank Account**: Convert the temporary account to an official corporate account
- **Bookkeeping**: Double-entry bookkeeping is mandatory under Article 112 of the Corporate Tax Act

## Visa for Foreign CEO/Representative Director

Foreign nationals serving as CEO of a Korean corporation must hold an appropriate visa:
- The **D-8 Corporate Investment Visa** is the standard requirement
- Requires KRW 100M+ investment and employment of 2+ Korean nationals
- Stay period: 1–3 years (renewable based on business performance)
