When a permit stalls or a rule out of line with international standards blocks your business, the public channel available to foreign investors and foreign-invested companies is the Foreign Investment Ombudsman. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act provides for appointing a Foreign Investment Ombudsman to handle the grievances of foreign investors and foreign-invested companies, and for a grievance office at the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) to support that work.

This guide is for the managers and administrators of foreign-invested companies doing business in Korea. Knowing which legal tools the Ombudsman actually has, where recommendation ends and where the outcome depends on the individual case, helps you present your grievance more precisely.

## Key Summary

- The Foreign Investment Ombudsman is chosen from people with extensive knowledge and experience in foreign investment affairs and appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Minister of Trade and Industry after deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee; the term is 3 years
- The Ombudsman can ask the heads of related administrative agencies and institutions for explanations or materials, statements from relevant staff and interested parties, and cooperation with site visits; the agency asked must comply unless there is a special reason
- Depending on the outcome, the Ombudsman can recommend improvements to the heads of related administrative agencies and public institutions, which must notify the results in writing within 30 days of receiving the recommendation
- If a recommendation is not carried out, the Ombudsman can ask for the matter to be put on the Foreign Investment Committee's agenda
- An annual report is submitted to the Foreign Investment Committee by the end of February of the following year
- Disclosing confidential information learned on the job is punishable
- The grievance office sits within KOTRA, is headed by the Ombudsman, and an agency that receives its request for cooperation must notify its results or opinion within 7 days

## 1. Who appoints the Foreign Investment Ombudsman, and how?

The Ombudsman is chosen from people with extensive knowledge and experience in foreign investment affairs. The appointment runs: recommendation by the Minister of Trade and Industry → deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee → appointment by the President. The term is 3 years.

The Ombudsman's duties are:

- investigating and handling grievances of foreign investors and foreign-invested companies
- preparing improvements to the foreign investment system and urging related administrative agencies and public institutions to implement them
- other matters needed to handle grievances

The Ombudsman may also attend the Foreign Investment Committee and state opinions, together with the head of KOTRA and project managers.

## 2. What can the Ombudsman ask other agencies for?

To handle a grievance, the Ombudsman can, where necessary, ask the heads of related administrative agencies and related institutions for the following cooperation. The head of the agency asked must comply unless there is a special reason.

| Request | Details |
|---|---|
| Explanation or materials | An explanation from the agency, or submission of materials under set criteria |
| Statements | Statements from relevant staff, interested parties and others |
| Site visits | Cooperation with site visits |

Materials can be requested on three grounds:

- where needed to examine whether a foreign investment rule fits international practice or standards
- where needed to resolve a foreign-invested company's business difficulties or to improve related rules
- where needed to improve the living conditions in Korea of foreign nationals working for foreign investors and foreign-invested companies

As the third ground shows, the scope covers not only company permit issues but also difficulties relating to the living conditions in Korea of foreign executives and employees.

## 3. Recommendations, the 30-day reply and the committee agenda request

Depending on the outcome of a grievance, the Ombudsman can, where necessary, recommend improvements to the heads of the related administrative agencies and public institutions. A recommendation is made in writing, stating specifically:

- the current state and problems of the relevant laws, systems and policies
- the content of the recommendation
- matters the Ombudsman considers necessary, such as the reply deadline for the agencies

The head of the agency receiving a recommendation must notify the Ombudsman of the results in writing within 30 days of receiving it. The Ombudsman can check and inspect how recommendations are being carried out, and if an agency does not carry out a recommendation, the Ombudsman can ask for the matter to be put on the Foreign Investment Committee's agenda.

The tools set out in the law are requests for cooperation, recommendations and agenda requests. The law does not give the Ombudsman power to cancel or change an agency's decision directly, so keep in mind that the final outcome on an individual decision still rests with the agency concerned.

## 4. The annual report: how individual grievances feed into system reform

The Ombudsman must prepare an annual report on improvement activities, including the current state of rules and systems that hinder foreign investment and the improvements achieved, and submit it to the Foreign Investment Committee by the end of February of the following year. The report includes:

- the rules and systems raised as grievances by foreign investors and foreign-invested companies
- the Ombudsman's improvements to rules and systems in the previous year
- other support activities for resolving grievances

So even a single company's grievance can become a reform item when many companies face the same problem.

## 5. Is confidentiality protected?

The Ombudsman must not use materials received from related agencies, or secrets learned in the course of duty, for purposes other than those set by the law, nor disclose them to others. Violation is punishable by imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 10 million won. The Ombudsman is also treated as a public official when certain penalty provisions of the Criminal Act are applied.

That said, handling a grievance means explaining the facts to the agencies involved, so decide at the filing stage how much company information may be shared.

## 6. How does the grievance office at KOTRA work?

A grievance office is set up within KOTRA to support the Ombudsman. It operates as follows:

- the head of the grievance office is the Foreign Investment Ombudsman
- when the head requests cooperation from a related administrative agency or institution, that body must notify its results or opinion within 7 days
- dedicated grievance officers can be designated by region and by invested company
- staff hearing statements or making site visits must carry identification showing their authority and show it to the persons concerned
- quarterly grievance results are analyzed and reported to the Minister of Trade and Industry within 1 month after the end of each quarter
- other organizational and operational matters are set by the head of KOTRA after deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee

The head of KOTRA may have seconded officials serve concurrently as dedicated grievance officers. Separately, foreign investment promotion offices in local governments and other agencies also receive, investigate and handle complaints or suggestions from foreign investors and foreign-invested companies, and must cooperate by submitting their status within 10 days after the end of each quarter when the head of the grievance office requests it.

## 7. What to organize before bringing a grievance

For the cooperation and recommendation procedures in the law to work, the problem has to be clear. The filing form and specific filing method need to be checked case by case, but it helps to prepare the following in advance:

- Company profile: whether registered as a foreign-invested company, business type, business location
- The agency and procedure involved: which application or decision at which agency got stuck, with a dated timeline
- Documents received: requests for supplementation, notices of return or refusal and other written notices from the agency
- Nature of the issue: a rule out of line with international standards, a business difficulty, or a living-conditions issue for foreign staff (organized along the three grounds for requesting materials)
- Desired outcome: an explanation, faster handling, or system improvement
- Sharing scope: which information may be disclosed to the agencies and which may not

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which body does the Foreign Investment Ombudsman belong to?

The Ombudsman is appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Minister of Trade and Industry after deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee, and the grievance office supporting its work sits within KOTRA. The head of the grievance office is the Ombudsman.

### Can the Ombudsman overturn an agency's decision?

The tools in the law are requests for cooperation to related agencies, recommendations, and, if a recommendation is not carried out, a request to put the matter on the Foreign Investment Committee's agenda. No power to change a decision directly is provided.

### By when must an agency respond to a recommendation?

It must notify the Ombudsman of the results in writing within 30 days of receiving the recommendation. When the head of the grievance office requests cooperation, the agency asked must notify its results or opinion within 7 days.

### Can it deal with stay or living issues of foreign employees?

The grounds for requesting materials include improving the living conditions in Korea of foreign nationals working for foreign investors and foreign-invested companies. How an individual issue is handled needs to be checked case by case.

### Could the company information we submit leak?

The Ombudsman must not use secrets learned on the job for purposes other than those set by the law or disclose them; violation is punishable by imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 10 million won.
