# Foreign Company Registration in Korea 2026 | Corporation vs LLC Guide

Korea actively welcomes foreign investment and allows foreigners to establish companies under the same framework as Korean nationals. This guide walks you through every step of the process.

## 1. Types of Business Entities for Foreign Investors

### Corporation (Jusik Hoesa / 주식회사)

The corporation is the most common business structure in Korea, equivalent to a joint-stock company. Shareholders bear liability only up to the amount of their investment.

- **Minimum capital**: Legally ₩100, but ₩100 million (approx. USD 75,000) is required to qualify as a foreign-invested enterprise under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA)
- **Directors**: Minimum 1 director (for small companies)
- **Auditor**: Waivable if capital is under ₩1 billion
- **Best for**: Large-scale investment, future IPO plans, multiple investors

### Limited Liability Company (Yuhan Hoesa / 유한회사)

The LLC is a simpler structure ideal for small-scale foreign investors. It offers more operational flexibility and a streamlined registration process.

- **Minimum capital**: ₩100 (practically determined by business needs)
- **Members**: 1 to 50
- **Directors**: Minimum 1 (CEO)
- **Best for**: Small investments, family-run businesses, simple structures

## 2. Pre-Registration Preparation

### Defining the Business Purpose

The company's business purpose must be clearly stated in the articles of incorporation. It is advisable to include a broad scope that covers potential future business activities to avoid costly amendments later.

### Choosing a Company Name

- Both Korean and English names are acceptable
- The same name cannot be used by two businesses of the same type in the same city/district
- Check name availability at the Supreme Court Internet Registry (www.iros.go.kr) before filing

### Preparing Capital

For recognition as a foreign investment under FIPA, a minimum of ₩100 million must be remitted from overseas. The capital must be deposited into an account opened in the incorporator's name, and a bank balance certificate must be obtained.

## 3. Foreign Investment Notification

Before incorporating, you must file a Foreign Investment Notification under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and FIPA.

### Filing Authorities

- **KOTRA (Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency)**: Available at Investment Promotion Centers nationwide
- **Foreign Exchange Banks**: Major banks such as KEB Hana Bank, Woori Bank

### Required Documents

1. Foreign Investment Notification Form
2. Copy of passport (foreign investor)
3. Business plan
4. Draft articles of incorporation

Upon approval, you will receive a Foreign Investment Enterprise Registration Certificate, which entitles you to tax incentives and administrative support.

## 4. Articles of Incorporation

The articles of incorporation are the company's supreme governing document and must include:

- Business purpose
- Company name
- Location of headquarters
- Total shares authorized and par value per share (for corporations)
- Name, address, and identification number of incorporators
- Terms of office for directors and auditors

For foreign investors, articles of incorporation require notarization. Foreign documents must be apostilled or consularly notarized.

## 5. Corporate Registration Procedure

### Required Documents for Registration

1. Application for corporate registration
2. Articles of incorporation (notarized)
3. Shareholder (or member) register
4. Minutes of incorporators' meeting (or general members' meeting)
5. Acceptance letters from directors and auditor
6. Capital payment receipt or bank balance certificate
7. Corporate seal registration form and seal card application

### Registration Steps

1. Visit the district court registry office or file online via the Internet Registry (www.iros.go.kr)
2. Pay the registration license tax (0.4% of capital, minimum ₩112,500)
3. After registration, obtain a certified copy of the corporate registry

## 6. Business Registration

After corporate registration, apply for business registration at the local tax office.

### Required Documents

1. Business registration application form (for corporations)
2. Certified copy of corporate registry
3. Copy of articles of incorporation
4. Lease agreement for business premises
5. Copy of CEO's ID

Business registration certificates are typically issued within 3–5 business days.

## 7. D-8 Visa Connection

Foreign investors can apply for the D-8 (Corporate Investment) visa after establishing their company in Korea.

### D-8 Visa Requirements

- Essential professional staff of a foreign-invested enterprise
- Investment of ₩100 million or more
- Korean business premises

### Required Documents for D-8 Visa

1. Visa application form
2. Copy of passport
3. Foreign Investment Enterprise Registration Certificate
4. Corporate business registration certificate
5. Certificate of employment or director registration confirmation

The D-8 visa is initially granted for 1–3 years and can be extended. Meeting certain conditions allows transition to F-2 (Resident) or F-5 (Permanent Resident) visa status.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can a foreigner own 100% of a Korean company?**

Yes. Korea allows 100% foreign ownership in most industries. However, certain sectors such as broadcasting, telecommunications, healthcare, and agriculture may have foreign equity restrictions.

**Q2. Can I register a company in Korea without being physically present?**

Yes. You can establish a company remotely through an authorized agent (legal scrivener or law firm). However, some steps such as the Foreign Investment Notification may require in-person attendance or notarized power of attorney.

**Q3. Does the CEO need to reside in Korea after incorporation?**

Not necessarily, but it is strongly recommended for operational efficiency and visa maintenance. If the CEO resides abroad, a domestic representative may need to be appointed.

**Q4. How do I open a corporate bank account as a foreigner?**

Visit a corporate banking branch with the certified copy of the corporate registry, business registration certificate, CEO's passport, and alien registration card. Some banks may request additional documentation for foreign CEOs.

**Q5. What benefits come with Foreign Investment Enterprise registration?**

Benefits include tax incentives (reduction in corporate tax and income tax), eligibility to enter designated foreign investment zones, KOTRA's one-stop support services, and streamlined administrative procedures.
