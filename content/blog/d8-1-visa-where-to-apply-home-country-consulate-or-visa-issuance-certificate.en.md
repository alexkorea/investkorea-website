When you prepare a Corporate Investment (D-8-1) visa from abroad, there are two routes. One is to apply for the visa directly at the Korean mission located, in principle, in your home country. The other is to apply for a Visa Issuance Certificate at the competent Korea Immigration Service office (office or branch) in Korea. A person who resides abroad and runs a business there (including long-term residents such as permanent residents) may also apply at the Korean mission in the country of residence rather than the home country.

This guide is for foreign investors who have finished, or are finishing, an investment in a Korean corporation, and for company staff in Korea who prepare documents on the investor's behalf. Which route fits depends on where the applicant is now and whether someone in Korea can handle the paperwork.

## Key Summary

- Consulate visa: in principle at the Korean mission in the home country (the mission in the country of residence is possible if you reside and run a business there)
- Visa Issuance Certificate: filed with the competent immigration office in Korea; a proxy adds a letter of authorization, the proxy's certificate of employment and the proxy's ID
- The requirements are the same on both routes: a fully established Korean corporation, an investment of one hundred million won or more, and ownership of 10 percent or more of the voting shares, or share ownership together with a contract to dispatch or appoint executives
- The core documents are nearly the same: foreign-invested company registration certificate, business registration, corporate register and the original statement of changes in shareholdings, and proof of investment fund inflow
- Individual investors below three hundred million won submit additional documents and are subject to close review
- Alien registration documents after entry can differ by route

## 1. How does a consulate visa differ from a Visa Issuance Certificate?

The biggest difference is where you file.

| Item | Visa at a Korean mission | Visa Issuance Certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Where to file | In principle, the Korean mission in the applicant's home country | Competent immigration office (office or branch) in Korea |
| Application form | Visa application form (Form No. 17) | Visa Issuance Certificate application form (Form No. 21) |
| Filing by proxy | No proxy item in the document list (check with the mission) | Letter of authorization, proxy's certificate of employment and proxy's ID added |
| Adjusting documents | The head of the mission may add or remove some documents when needed | The head of the immigration office may add or remove some documents |

Think of the consulate route as the applicant dealing with the mission directly, and the certificate route as a company or proxy in Korea filing the papers. Note that the consulate route for D-8-1 carries the qualifier "excluding specified countries", so whether the applicant's nationality falls under it needs to be checked case by case.

## 2. Where do you apply if you live outside your home country?

A consulate application is in principle made at the Korean mission in the applicant's home country. However, if the applicant resides abroad and runs a business there (including long-term residents such as permanent residents), the application can be made at the Korean mission in the country of residence.

For example, an investor who is a national of country A but has held permanent residency and run a company in country B would look at the Korean mission in country B. If you are only staying in country B for a short time, whether this exception applies may be judged differently, so it helps to prepare records showing your residence and business operation in advance.

## 3. Which documents are common to both routes?

Apart from the application form, the documents for both routes are nearly identical.

- For a posting: a dispatch order (stating the posting period) and a certificate of employment
- A copy of the foreign-invested company registration certificate
- A copy of the business registration certificate, the corporate register and the original statement of changes in shareholdings
- Proof of investment fund inflow
  - Cash contribution: a foreign currency export permit (report) from the customs of the country concerned or a home-country bank (financial institution), where applicable, and a statement of fund inflow (remittance confirmation, certificate of foreign exchange purchase, customs declaration, etc.)
  - Contribution in kind: a copy of the in-kind contribution completion certificate (issued by the Commissioner of the Korea Customs Service) and a copy of the customs import declaration certificate

The consulate route adds a visa application form, passport, 1 standard photo and the fee; the certificate route adds a Visa Issuance Certificate application form, passport and 1 standard photo. Check the fee amount with the office where you file.

## 4. What changes if the investment is below three hundred million won?

Individual investors below three hundred million won submit additional documents on both routes.

- Proof of how the capital was used (purchase receipts, office interior costs, Korean bank account statements, etc.)
- Proof that the business premises exist (office lease, photos of the premises, workspace and signboard, etc.)
- Documents from the country of nationality on business experience in the relevant industry or field (requested when needed)

Small individual investors below three hundred million won are also subject to close review. Switching routes does not change this standard, so whichever route you choose, prepare records showing the premises and the use of capital to the same level.

## 5. Is there a difference at alien registration after entry?

Yes. A foreigner who received Corporate Investment (D-8) status directly from a Korean mission and entered submits, at alien registration, the documents required for a change of status application, applied mutatis mutandis. A person who obtained the visa through a Visa Issuance Certificate is excluded from this.

In other words, if you received the visa directly at a mission, you need to bring change-of-status-level documents at the registration stage after entry. The basic alien registration documents include the business registration certificate and the corporate register (for corporations); for those who entered with a Visa Issuance Certificate, exactly how much must be submitted should be confirmed with the competent office case by case.

## 6. Common problems

- Applying at a mission outside the home country without showing residence and business operation there
- A proxy filing for a Visa Issuance Certificate without the letter of authorization or the proxy's certificate of employment
- Rushing to apply before the corporation is fully established (only fully established corporations qualify as the investment target)
- Visiting for alien registration without knowing that change-of-status-level documents are needed after receiving the visa at a mission
- Investing below three hundred million won without preparing proof of premises and capital use

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must a D-8-1 visa be obtained only at the home-country mission?

The principle is the Korean mission in the applicant's home country, but a person who resides abroad and runs a business there (including long-term residents such as permanent residents) can also apply at the mission in the country of residence.

### Can a staff member in Korea apply on my behalf?

A Visa Issuance Certificate is filed with the competent immigration office (office or branch) in Korea, and a proxy additionally submits a letter of authorization, the proxy's certificate of employment and the proxy's ID.

### Are the investment requirements different between the routes?

No. You must invest one hundred million won or more in a fully established Korean corporation and own 10 percent or more of the voting shares, or own shares and sign a contract to dispatch or appoint executives.

### Can they ask for more documents?

The head of a Korean mission or of an immigration office may add or remove some documents to review the genuineness of the invitation and the qualifications involved.

### How long a stay can be granted?

For activity at a foreign-invested company, the upper limit of the stay period is 5 years. The period actually granted can vary with the review.
