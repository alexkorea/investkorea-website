Yes. Besides owning shares or equity, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act treats a loan of five years or more extended to a foreign-invested company by its overseas parent company, a foreign investor and related companies as foreign investment. Not every loan from a foreign company qualifies: the lender must have a relationship defined by law, and the five years are judged by the loan period set in the initial loan agreement.

This guide is for finance staff at overseas headquarters who plan to send operating or equipment funds to a Korean subsidiary as a loan, and for staff of foreign-invested companies.

## Key Summary

- Loan-type foreign investment = a loan of five years or more extended to a foreign-invested company (based on the loan period set in the initial loan agreement)
- The lender must be the overseas parent, a company with a capital relationship with the parent, a foreign investor, or a company with a capital relationship with the foreign investor
- The capital relationship is defined in the Enforcement Decree using thresholds such as 50% or more and 10% or more of shares
- Loan-type investment is reported in advance on a separate form, and a change report can be filed when the lender, amount or terms change
- With installment or early repayments, the loan period is calculated by weighting each repayment
- Principal, interest and fees paid under the loan agreement are guaranteed to be remitted abroad according to the report
- Tax reduction provisions also apply to the lender of the loan unless a law provides otherwise

## 1. Who must the lender be for the loan to count as foreign investment?

The Foreign Investment Promotion Act treats a loan of five years or more extended to the foreign-invested company by any of the following as foreign investment.

| Lender | Description |
|---|---|
| Overseas parent | The overseas parent company of the foreign-invested company |
| Company related to the parent | A company with a capital relationship with the overseas parent as defined by Presidential Decree |
| Foreign investor | A foreigner who owns shares or equity, or has made a contribution, under the Act |
| Company related to the foreign investor | A company with a capital relationship with the foreign investor as defined by Presidential Decree |

The borrower is the foreign-invested company. A Korean company with no foreign shareholding that borrows from an overseas business partner does not fall under this type.

## 2. How is a capital relationship determined?

The Enforcement Decree defines a company with a capital relationship with the overseas parent as either of the following.

- A company owning 50% or more of the total issued shares or total capital contribution of the overseas parent
- Where the overseas parent owns 50% or more of the foreign-invested company: a company owning 10% or more of the overseas parent, or a company 50% or more owned by the overseas parent or by the company above

A company with a capital relationship with the foreign investor is a company that is 50% or more owned by a foreign investor that itself owns 50% or more of the foreign-invested company. When another group affiliate lends the funds, it is best to draw the shareholding chart first and check which threshold applies.

## 3. How are the five years calculated?

Whether the loan is five years or more is judged by the loan period set in the initial loan agreement. The Enforcement Rule requires the loan period on the loan-type report form to be calculated taking into account both the grace period and the repayment period.

For installment or early repayments (including prepayment), the repayment period is the sum of each installment's repayment period multiplied by that installment's share of the total loan amount. Even if the contract matures in five years, a structure that repays a large amount early can shorten the calculated period, so the repayment schedule should be reviewed together.

## 4. Where and how is the loan reported?

A foreigner who intends to make a foreign investment must report it in advance. Receiving reports and issuing report certificates is entrusted to the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and the heads of foreign exchange banks. Loan-type foreign investment is reported on a separate form (Form No. 2) different from share acquisition, and the same form is used for change reports. Once the report is received, a report certificate is issued to the reporting person without delay.

Note that the Enforcement Decree's thresholds of an investment amount of KRW 100 million or more and 10% or more of voting shares apply to the share ownership type. The loan type is defined by the lender scope and loan period above, and the specific attachments need to be confirmed case by case.

## 5. What if the loan is repaid early or the terms change?

If the lender, loan amount or loan terms in the report change, a change report reflecting the new details can be filed. When filing the change report, the loan amount is calculated excluding the repaid amount from the month following the month in which the repayment date falls.

If the change report includes early repayment of the loan, the details are notified to the Commissioner of the National Tax Service, the Commissioner of the Korea Customs Service and the relevant metropolitan or provincial governor. Since early repayment may be linked to taxes or foreign investment zone benefits, it is safer to check existing reduction and support conditions before repaying early.

## 6. Can principal and interest be sent abroad freely? What about tax reductions?

Principal, interest and fees paid under the loan agreement are guaranteed to be remitted abroad according to the report or permit details at the time of remittance. The head of the foreign exchange bank must check whether the lender's remittance is legitimate and may ask the entrusted reporting agency to confirm the foreign investment information if needed. If the reported details differ from the actual contract terms, checks at the remittance stage can take longer.

In addition, tax reduction provisions that apply to Korean companies also apply to the lender of the loan unless a law provides otherwise. Which reductions are available needs to be confirmed under the individual tax law provisions.

## 7. Common problems

- Preparing the report without checking the shareholding thresholds when the lender is a group affiliate
- Judging the loan to be five years or more from the maturity date alone, when installment repayments shorten the calculated period
- Not filing a change report after early repayment, so the reported balance differs from the actual balance
- Assuming that a loan taken from abroad by a company that is not a foreign-invested company is loan-type foreign investment
- Treating recognition of a loan as investment funds in a D-8 status review as the same question as the foreign investment report (the status criteria must be checked separately with the immigration office)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is every loan from an overseas parent foreign investment?

No. It must be a loan to a foreign-invested company with a loan period of five years or more set in the initial agreement, and the lender must be the overseas parent, a foreign investor or a company with a capital relationship with them.

### Can a group affiliate be the lender?

An affiliate can be the lender if it has a capital relationship defined in the Enforcement Decree, such as owning 50% or more of the overseas parent. The answer depends on the shareholding structure.

### What happens if part of the loan is repaid early?

The loan period is recalculated by weighting the repayments, and a change report can be filed when the amount or terms change. Early repayment details in a change report are notified to the tax and customs authorities and others.

### Can interest and principal be sent abroad?

Principal, interest and fees under the loan agreement are guaranteed to be remitted according to the report or permit details, and the foreign exchange bank checks the legitimacy of the remittance.

### Does the KRW 100 million threshold apply to loans?

The KRW 100 million and 10% thresholds in the Enforcement Decree apply to the share ownership type. The specific reporting requirements and documents for the loan type need to be confirmed case by case.
