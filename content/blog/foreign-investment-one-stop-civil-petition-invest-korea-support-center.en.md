To a large extent, yes. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act establishes a Foreign Investment Support Center at the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency to comprehensively carry out consultation, guidance, promotion, surveys and research related to foreign investment, the processing and filing of civil petitions on behalf of applicants, business incubation and more. Foreign investors and foreign-invested companies can ask the Center to act for them in civil petitions, such as preparing and submitting application documents, and for certain petitions a permit is deemed granted if no refusal is notified within the set processing period.

This guide is for foreign investors and staff of foreign-invested companies who need several permits to build a factory or start a business. Knowing the processing period rules makes scheduling easier.

## 1. Where is the Foreign Investment Support Center, and what does it do?

The Foreign Investment Support Center is located at the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency. Its statutory tasks are consultation, guidance, promotion, surveys and research related to foreign investment, the processing and filing of civil petitions on behalf of applicants, business incubation, and other support for foreign investors and foreign-invested companies.

In addition, receiving foreign investment reports and change reports, issuing report certificates, and registering foreign-invested companies and changes are entrusted to the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency and the heads of foreign exchange banks. The structure allows you to go through the same body from the investment report through registration and subsequent permit petitions.

## 2. Are officials from relevant agencies dispatched to the Center?

The head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency may, when necessary, ask relevant administrative agencies and foreign investment-related corporations or organizations (related organizations) to dispatch public officials or staff to work at the Center. When requesting the dispatch of public officials, the head must consult the competent minister in advance.

The Center is run mainly by staff of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency, and the dispatched officials and staff (dispatched officers) support the Center's work. Dispatched officers are under the direction and supervision of the head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency with respect to their service.

The head of the Agency may, when necessary, ask the heads of relevant administrative agencies to set up a branch office of their agency within the Center, and the heads receiving the request must comply unless there is a special reason.

## 3. What does a project manager do?

The head of the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency may designate a project manager for each foreign investor or foreign-invested company from among Agency staff, dispatched officers, or officials or staff of relevant central administrative agencies, local governments and public institutions, and must notify the foreign investor and foreign-invested company of the designation. The project manager's tasks are:

- Collecting and providing materials and information and arranging meetings at the request of the foreign investor or foreign-invested company
- Giving opinions on foreign investment support such as tax reduction, lease of state and public property and cash grants
- Supporting the Center's work and acting on civil petitions
- Supporting the settlement of staff and their families, such as guidance on renting housing and school admission

Relevant administrative agencies that have been notified must actively cooperate when the project manager requests cooperation on providing materials, processing civil petitions and so on.

## 4. How does petition filing work? Batch, individual and direct processing

Foreign investors or foreign-invested companies can ask the Center to act for them in civil petitions, such as preparing and submitting application documents. The head of the Center then transfers the following petitions to the head of the processing agency for processing and informs the competent foreign investment promotion officer.

| Type | Details |
|---|---|
| Batch-processed petitions | Petitions for permits listed in an annexed table of the Act; a structure in which one permit is deemed to include related permits |
| Individually processed petitions | Foreign investment-related petitions processed individually, listed in an annexed table of the Act |
| Directly processed petitions | Petitions set by Presidential Decree, processed directly by dispatched officers |

For directly processed petitions, the head of the agency to which the dispatched officer belongs must delegate final decision authority to that officer. Which permits each annexed table contains needs to be confirmed case by case. To obtain permits, the application documents set by Ordinance of the Ministry of Trade and Industry must be submitted.

## 5. How is the processing period protected? Deemed permits

Notwithstanding other laws, the head of the processing agency or the dispatched officer must process batch, individual and direct petitions within the processing period set by Presidential Decree. If no refusal of the permit is notified within that period, the permit is deemed granted on the day after the processing period ends.

| Type | Processing period |
|---|---|
| Directly processed petitions | The period set in an annexed table of the Enforcement Decree |
| Batch-processed petitions | The period set in an annexed table of the Enforcement Decree (if a related permit petition is filed separately, the processing period under the relevant law) |
| Individually processed petitions | The processing period under the relevant law |

The processing period is counted from the day the head of the processing agency or the dispatched officer receives the petition. Time spent on supplementing or correcting the application is not counted. If there is a reasonable and objective reason, the processing period can be extended only once, and the applicant (or the head of the Center if the Center is acting) must be notified of the reason and the expected additional period. If a relevant agency asked for consultation does not submit an opinion within the processing period, it is deemed to have no opinion.

When a permit is deemed granted, the head of the processing agency and the dispatched officer must, upon application by the foreign investor, issue without delay a document certifying that the permit was granted. The specific number of days in the annexed tables needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 6. What if the petition is refused?

When notifying a refusal within the processing period, the reasons must be given in writing to the foreign investment promotion officer and to the foreign investor or foreign-invested company, specifying the grounds for refusal and their legal basis.

If a foreign investor that received a refusal submits documents proving it has resolved the grounds for refusal and meets the permit requirements, the head of the processing agency or the dispatched officer must grant the original permit within 3 days. In doing so, the permit cannot be refused on grounds other than the original grounds for refusal. It is important to resolve each ground stated in the notice one by one.

## 7. Conditional permits, foreign investment promotion offices and common problems

Even if some requirements, such as attachments, are not met for a batch-processed petition, the head of the processing agency may grant the permit on condition that they be supplemented. The time for supplementing is set according to the type of permit, such as when applying for a building permit, when reporting the start of construction, when reporting the start of operation, or when registering in the building register, and the holder of a conditional permit must submit a confirmation that the condition has been fulfilled when applying for the permit at that stage.

Foreign investment promotion offices, designated or established in central administrative agencies, metropolitan and provincial governments and city, county and district governments, also encourage and check the processing of transferred petitions and act on petitions.

Common problems:

- Thinking that going through the Center means permits are issued without meeting the requirements (the permit requirements under the relevant laws must still be met)
- Planning the schedule on the assumption that time spent on supplementing or correcting counts toward the processing period
- Delaying resubmission after a refusal by fixing unrelated parts as well
- Omitting the confirmation of fulfilling the conditions of a conditional permit at the next stage
