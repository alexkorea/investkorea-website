Contributing money to a nonprofit corporation, without buying any shares, can also be foreign investment. The Foreign Investment Promotion Act lists as methods of foreign investment a contribution by a foreign national to a nonprofit corporation in the science and technology field that meets set criteria, made to establish a continuing cooperative relationship, and any other contribution to a nonprofit corporation that the Foreign Investment Committee recognizes as foreign investment. In both cases the foreign national's contribution must be at least 50 million won and at least 10/100 of the total contributions.

This guide is for overseas companies and research institutions that want to work with Korean research institutes, foundations and other nonprofits, and for nonprofits planning to receive foreign contributions. Once a contribution is recognized as foreign investment, the contributing foreign national becomes a "foreign investor" and the receiving corporation becomes a "nonprofit corporation that has received a contribution", and a registration obligation arises.

## Key Summary

- Two routes count as foreign investment: (1) a contribution to a science and technology nonprofit made to establish a continuing cooperative relationship; (2) any other contribution to a nonprofit that the Foreign Investment Committee recognizes as foreign investment
- Nonprofit criteria for route (1): an independent research facility, plus either at least 5 full-time dedicated researchers or carrying out research and development in natural sciences and engineering
- Nonprofit criteria for route (2): a nonprofit set up to promote academia, arts, medicine, education and the like that continuously trains specialists and expands international exchange, or a regional headquarters of an international organization carrying out international cooperation projects
- Amount threshold (both routes): the foreign national's contribution is at least 50 million won and at least 10/100 of the total contributions
- As a rule, foreign investment is reported to the Minister of Trade and Industry in advance
- Once the contribution is completed, register as a foreign-invested company within 60 days from the date the reason arises; under route (1), if the nonprofit does not yet meet the criteria after the contribution, within 30 days from the date it meets them

## 1. When does a contribution count as foreign investment?

Besides acquiring shares or equity and long-term loans, the methods of foreign investment include contributions to nonprofit corporations. There are two routes.

| | (1) Science and technology nonprofit | (2) Committee-recognized contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient | A nonprofit corporation that is a Korean corporation or enterprise in the science and technology field and meets the research staff and facility criteria | A nonprofit corporation meeting criteria on its business activities and the like |
| Purpose / decision | To establish a continuing cooperative relationship with that corporation | Recognized as foreign investment by the Foreign Investment Committee |
| Amount | At least 10/100 of the total contributions and at least 50 million won | At least 50 million won and at least 10/100 of the total contributions |

Route (1) counts as foreign investment when the criteria in the regulations are met; route (2) counts only when the Foreign Investment Committee recognizes it. Matters on contributions to nonprofit corporations are also among the items the Foreign Investment Committee deliberates on.

## 2. Which criteria must a science and technology nonprofit meet?

A nonprofit receiving a route (1) contribution must meet all of the following:

1. It has an independent research facility
2. It meets one of the following
   - at least 5 full-time employees who are dedicated researchers holding a bachelor's degree in science and technology with at least 3 years of research experience, or a master's degree or higher in science and technology
   - it carries out research and development in natural sciences and engineering under the Korean Standard Industrial Classification

If the research facility is shared with another institution, or the researchers are not full-time employees, check separately whether the criteria are met. The exact scope of recognition needs to be checked case by case.

## 3. Which corporations qualify for a contribution recognized by the Foreign Investment Committee?

For route (2), the nonprofit receiving the contribution must be one of the following:

- a nonprofit set up to promote academia, arts, medicine, education and the like that continuously carries out projects to train specialists in its field and expand international exchange
- a regional headquarters of an international organization carrying out private or intergovernmental international cooperation projects

Even then, the Foreign Investment Committee must recognize it as foreign investment, so plan for the time the committee's deliberation takes.

## 4. How large must the contribution be?

Under both routes, the foreign national's contribution must be at least 50 million won and at least 10/100 of the total contributions. Both tests must be met, so if the nonprofit's total contributions are large, the 10/100 test may require more than 50 million won.

Before contributing, work out the nonprofit's total contributions and what the foreign share will be after this contribution. At which point in time the total is measured needs to be checked case by case.

## 5. What do "foreign investor" and "nonprofit corporation that has received a contribution" mean?

The law defines the terms as follows:

- Foreign investor: a foreign national who owns shares or equity, or who has made a contribution, under the Act
- Foreign-invested company or nonprofit corporation that has received a contribution: a company in which a foreign investor has invested, or a nonprofit corporation to which a foreign investor has contributed

So the contributing foreign national becomes a foreign investor even without holding shares, and the receiving nonprofit is covered by the law alongside foreign-invested companies.

## 6. How do reporting and registration after the contribution work?

As a rule, a foreign national who intends to make foreign investment by one of the listed methods must report it to the Minister of Trade and Industry in advance. Once the contribution is completed, the foreign-invested company registration must be made.

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| Report | Report to the Minister of Trade and Industry in advance when intending to make foreign investment |
| Reason for registration | The contribution has been completed |
| Deadline | Within 60 days from the date the reason arises |
| Exception | Under route (1), if the nonprofit does not meet the research facility and staff criteria after the contribution, within 30 days from the date it meets them |

The authority to receive reports and change reports, issue report certificates, and handle foreign-invested company registration and change registration is delegated to the head of KOTRA and the heads of foreign exchange banks. The specific documents, such as the contribution agreement, remittance records and the nonprofit's research facility and staff records, need to be checked case by case.

## 7. If the nonprofit expands its research facilities

The rules on cash grants for foreign investment include, as one eligible case, a route (1) nonprofit newly installing or expanding a research facility (together with requirements such as at least 5 full-time dedicated researchers in the relevant field). The grant amount is set through negotiation with the foreign national and deliberation by the Foreign Investment Committee, so eligibility and amount need to be checked case by case.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a contribution that brings no shares still foreign investment?

Yes. A contribution to a science and technology nonprofit made to establish a continuing cooperative relationship, and a contribution to a nonprofit that the Foreign Investment Committee recognizes as foreign investment, are listed methods of foreign investment. The contributing foreign national becomes a foreign investor.

### What is the amount threshold?

The foreign national's contribution must be at least 50 million won and at least 10/100 of the total contributions. Both tests must be met.

### Which nonprofits qualify without separate committee recognition?

A science and technology nonprofit with an independent research facility and either at least 5 full-time dedicated researchers or research and development in natural sciences and engineering, where the contribution is made for continuing cooperation. Contributions to other nonprofits need the Foreign Investment Committee's recognition.

### By when must registration be made after contributing?

Register as a foreign-invested company within 60 days of completing the contribution. If a science and technology nonprofit does not meet the criteria after the contribution, register within 30 days from the date it meets them.

### Where are reports and registrations filed?

The authority lies with the Minister of Trade and Industry, and receiving reports and registering foreign-invested companies are delegated to the head of KOTRA and the heads of foreign exchange banks.
