# Foreigner Real Estate Purchase in Korea | Complete Guide to Buying Property

Korea generally permits foreigners to acquire real estate, but the rules vary depending on the type of property and the purpose of acquisition. This guide provides a practical overview of everything you need to know before purchasing property in Korea.

## 1. What Foreigners Can Buy in Korea

### Permitted Property Types

- **Apartments and condominiums**: Freely purchasable by foreigners
- **Single-family homes and commercial buildings**: Permitted
- **Land**: Mostly permitted, though certain areas require special authorization

### Restricted Areas for Land Acquisition

In the following cases, additional permits or notifications are required:

- **Military facility protection zones**: Requires approval from the Minister of National Defense
- **Cultural heritage protection zones**: Requires approval from the Cultural Heritage Administration
- **Agricultural land (farmland)**: Requires a Farmland Acquisition Qualification Certificate — practically difficult for non-resident foreigners
- **Forest land**: Subject to separate notification requirements

## 2. Reporting Obligations

### Real Estate Transaction Reporting

Since 2017, all real estate transactions must be reported to the local municipal office within 30 days of the contract date, under the Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act.

- Reporting parties: Both buyer and seller (typically handled by the licensed real estate agent)
- Reporting method: In-person or online via the Real Estate Transaction Management System (RTMS)

### Foreign Buyer Notification

Under the Foreigner Land Act, foreigners who acquire Korean real estate must file a separate notification with the local municipal office within **60 days** of the acquisition date.

- Applies to: All real estate acquisitions that include land
- Penalty for non-reporting: Up to 10% of the acquisition price (maximum ₩5 million)

## 3. Required Documents

### Basic Documents

1. Original purchase contract
2. Copy of passport and alien registration card (if applicable)
3. Foreigner Real Estate Acquisition Notification Form
4. Acquisition tax payment receipt
5. Seal certificate or signature verification document

### Additional Documents for Overseas Residents

- Notarized and apostilled home-country ID documents
- Documents confirmed by the nearest Korean consulate or embassy (if required)

## 4. Step-by-Step Purchase Process

### Step 1: Property Search and Real Estate Agent

It is strongly recommended to use a licensed real estate agent (공인중개사) when buying property in Korea. They will verify the property registry, analyze ownership and lien status, and assist with contract preparation.

### Step 2: Review the Property Registry

Before signing any contract, always review the certified copy of the property registry (등기사항전부증명서):

- **Section A (갑구)**: Ownership-related information — check for seizures, attachments, and auction filings
- **Section B (을구)**: Encumbrances such as mortgages and lease rights

### Step 3: Sign the Contract and Pay the Deposit

- Deposit: Typically 5–10% of the purchase price
- The contract should specify the total price, payment schedule, balance date, and handover conditions
- Foreign buyers may be required to provide documentation on the source of funds

### Step 4: Intermediate and Final Payments

- Intermediate payment: Paid within an agreed period after contract signing (may be omitted)
- Final payment (balance): Paid on the closing date in exchange for ownership transfer

### Step 5: Transfer of Ownership Registration

Ownership transfer registration is filed at the local district court registry simultaneously with the final payment. This is typically handled by a legal scrivener (법무사) acting on your behalf.

## 5. Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting

If you are remitting funds from overseas to purchase Korean real estate, foreign exchange reporting is required under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

### Reporting Requirements

- **Overseas remittance**: Automatically recorded when funds are transferred through a foreign exchange bank
- **Source of funds**: Documentation required for large transactions (typically ₩500 million or more)
- **Offshore fund inflow**: Remittances exceeding USD 50,000 per year must be reported to the Bank of Korea

### Practical Tips

- Clearly state "real estate acquisition funds" as the purpose of transfer
- Consult with the bank's foreign exchange officer in advance

## 6. Acquisition Tax

### Acquisition Tax Rates

Acquisition tax is paid to local governments under the Local Tax Act upon purchase.

| Category | Tax Rate |
|----------|----------|
| Housing (under ₩600M, 1 household / 1 home) | 1% |
| Housing (₩600M–₩900M) | 1–3% |
| Housing (over ₩900M or multiple homes) | 3–12% |
| Commercial real estate | 4% |
| Land | 4% |

Foreigners are subject to the same rates as Korean nationals. However, heavy tax regulations for multi-home owners and speculative zone purchases have been strengthened since 2020.

### Additional Taxes

In addition to acquisition tax, a Special Rural Development Tax (10% of acquisition tax) and a Local Education Tax (20% of acquisition tax) are assessed.

## 7. Mortgage Options for Foreigners

### Can Foreigners Get a Mortgage?

Yes, foreigners can generally apply for home mortgage loans from Korean banks. However, eligibility and terms vary based on visa type and length of stay.

### Key Conditions

- **Visa status**: Long-term visa holders (D-7, D-8, E-7, F-2, F-4, F-5, F-6, etc.)
- **Proof of income**: Employment contract, tax payment records
- **LTV (Loan-to-Value ratio)**: 40–70% depending on region and property type
- **DSR (Debt Service Ratio)**: Annual principal and interest repayments must not exceed 40% of income (in speculative overheating zones)

### Cases Where Mortgage May Be Difficult

- Short-term visa holders (C-3, B-1 tourist/visitor visa)
- No verifiable income in Korea
- No credit history in Korea

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can foreigners purchase apartments in speculative overheating zones?**

Yes, acquisition is permitted, but stronger regulations apply — including heavy taxes for multi-home buyers, tighter LTV limits, and resale restrictions. A Funding Plan Report is mandatory for purchases of ₩300 million or more in speculative overheating zones.

**Q2. Can I buy Korean real estate while living abroad?**

Yes. You can visit Korea to complete the transaction in person, or authorize a legal scrivener to handle the ownership transfer registration on your behalf through a power of attorney. Foreign exchange transaction reporting must also be carefully prepared.

**Q3. How is rental income taxed for foreigners?**

Rental income is subject to comprehensive income tax at rates of 6–45%. For residential rental income, amounts up to ₩20 million annually can be taxed at a flat 14% rate using separate taxation.

**Q4. Does owning property in Korea help with visa applications?**

Owning real estate alone does not directly qualify you for a Korean visa. However, for the D-8 (Corporate Investment) visa, establishing a business and securing a Korean base — which may include a property — can be advantageous.

**Q5. How is capital gains tax calculated when I sell?**

Foreigners are subject to capital gains tax (양도소득세) at the same rates as Korean nationals — ranging from 6% to 75% depending on the holding period and number of properties owned. A 1-household / 1-home owner who has held the property for at least 2 years may qualify for a tax exemption on the portion up to ₩900 million.
